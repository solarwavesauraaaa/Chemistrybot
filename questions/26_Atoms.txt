# 26_Atoms

Q: The first model of the atom was proposed by:
A) J.J. Thomson
B) Rutherford
C) Bohr
D) Dalton
Answer: A [Thomson proposed the plum pudding model.]

Q: Rutherford's alpha particle scattering experiment led to the discovery of:
A) The nucleus
B) The electron
C) The proton
D) The neutron
Answer: A [Rutherford's experiment led to the discovery of the atomic nucleus.]

Q: The nucleus of an atom consists of:
A) Protons and neutrons
B) Protons and electrons
C) Electrons and neutrons
D) Only protons
Answer: A [The nucleus contains protons and neutrons (nucleons).]

Q: The charge on the nucleus is:
A) Positive
B) Negative
C) Neutral
D) Zero
Answer: A [Protons give the nucleus a positive charge.]

Q: The size of the nucleus is of the order of:
A) 10^-15 m
B) 10^-10 m
C) 10^-9 m
D) 10^-12 m
Answer: A [Nuclear radius is ~10^-15 m (femtometer).]

Q: The radius of the atom is of the order of:
A) 10^-10 m
B) 10^-15 m
C) 10^-9 m
D) 10^-12 m
Answer: A [Atomic radius is ~10^-10 m (Angstrom).]

Q: The ratio of the radius of the atom to the radius of the nucleus is:
A) 10^5
B) 10^3
C) 10^7
D) 10^2
Answer: A [10^-10 / 10^-15 = 10^5.]

Q: Rutherford's model of the atom was:
A) Planetary model
B) Plum pudding model
C) Quantum model
D) Wave mechanical model
Answer: A [Rutherford's model is like a planetary system.]

Q: The main drawback of Rutherford's model was:
A) It could not explain the stability of the atom
B) It could not explain the nucleus
C) It could not explain electrons
D) It could not explain protons
Answer: A [Rutherford's model failed to explain why electrons don't fall into the nucleus.]

Q: Bohr's model of the atom was based on:
A) Quantum theory
B) Classical theory
C) Relativity theory
D) Wave theory
Answer: A [Bohr combined classical and quantum concepts.]

Q: In Bohr's model, the electron revolves around the nucleus in:
A) Circular orbits
B) Elliptical orbits
C) Parabolic orbits
D) Random paths
Answer: A [Bohr orbits are circular.]

Q: The angular momentum of an electron in a Bohr orbit is quantized as:
A) L = n h / 2pi
B) L = n h
C) L = h / 2pi n
D) L = n h / 4pi
Answer: A [L = n h / 2pi.]

Q: The energy of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom is:
A) E_n = -13.6 / n^2 eV
B) E_n = 13.6 / n^2 eV
C) E_n = -13.6 n^2 eV
D) E_n = 13.6 n^2 eV
Answer: A [E_n = -13.6 / n^2 eV.]

Q: The radius of the nth orbit in Bohr's model is:
A) r_n = 0.529 n^2 A
B) r_n = 0.529 n A
C) r_n = 0.529 / n^2 A
D) r_n = 0.529 / n A
Answer: A [r_n = 0.529 n^2 A.]

Q: The velocity of an electron in the nth orbit is:
A) v_n = 2.18 × 10^6 / n m/s
B) v_n = 2.18 × 10^6 n m/s
C) v_n = 2.18 × 10^6 / n^2 m/s
D) v_n = 2.18 × 10^6 n^2 m/s
Answer: A [v_n = 2.18 × 10^6 / n m/s.]

Q: The frequency of revolution of an electron in the nth orbit is:
A) f_n = v_n / 2pi r_n
B) f_n = 2pi r_n / v_n
C) f_n = v_n * 2pi r_n
D) f_n = v_n / r_n
Answer: A [f = v / 2pi r.]

Q: The energy of the first excited state of hydrogen is:
A) -3.4 eV
B) -13.6 eV
C) -1.51 eV
D) -0.85 eV
Answer: A [E2 = -13.6 / 4 = -3.4 eV.]

Q: The energy of the second excited state of hydrogen is:
A) -1.51 eV
B) -3.4 eV
C) -13.6 eV
D) -0.85 eV
Answer: A [E3 = -13.6 / 9 = -1.51 eV.]

Q: The ionization energy of hydrogen is:
A) 13.6 eV
B) 1.36 eV
C) 136 eV
D) 13.6 MeV
Answer: A [Ionization energy = 13.6 eV.]

Q: The Lyman series of hydrogen spectrum lies in the:
A) Ultraviolet region
B) Visible region
C) Infrared region
D) X-ray region
Answer: A [Lyman series is in the UV region.]

Q: The Balmer series of hydrogen spectrum lies in the:
A) Visible region
B) Ultraviolet region
C) Infrared region
D) X-ray region
Answer: A [Balmer series is in the visible region.]

Q: The Paschen series of hydrogen spectrum lies in the:
A) Infrared region
B) Visible region
C) Ultraviolet region
D) X-ray region
Answer: A [Paschen series is in the infrared region.]

Q: The Brackett series of hydrogen spectrum lies in the:
A) Infrared region
B) Visible region
C) Ultraviolet region
D) X-ray region
Answer: A [Brackett series is in the infrared region.]

Q: The Pfund series of hydrogen spectrum lies in the:
A) Infrared region
B) Visible region
C) Ultraviolet region
D) X-ray region
Answer: A [Pfund series is in the infrared region.]

Q: The Rydberg formula for the wavelength of spectral lines is:
A) 1/lambda = R (1/n1^2 - 1/n2^2)
B) 1/lambda = R (1/n2^2 - 1/n1^2)
C) lambda = R (1/n1^2 - 1/n2^2)
D) lambda = R (1/n2^2 - 1/n1^2)
Answer: A [1/lambda = R (1/n1^2 - 1/n2^2).]

Q: The value of the Rydberg constant (R) is:
A) 1.097 × 10^7 m^-1
B) 1.097 × 10^9 m^-1
C) 1.097 × 10^5 m^-1
D) 1.097 × 10^11 m^-1
Answer: A [R = 1.097 × 10^7 m^-1.]

Q: The shortest wavelength in the Lyman series is:
A) 91.2 nm
B) 121.6 nm
C) 656.3 nm
D) 1875 nm
Answer: A [n1=1, n2=∞ gives lambda = 1/R = 91.2 nm.]

Q: The longest wavelength in the Lyman series is:
A) 121.6 nm
B) 91.2 nm
C) 656.3 nm
D) 1875 nm
Answer: A [n1=1, n2=2 gives lambda = 1/(R(1 - 1/4)) = 4/(3R) = 121.6 nm.]

Q: The longest wavelength in the Balmer series is:
A) 656.3 nm
B) 121.6 nm
C) 91.2 nm
D) 1875 nm
Answer: A [n1=2, n2=3 gives lambda = 1/(R(1/4 - 1/9)) = 1/(R*5/36) = 36/(5R) = 656.3 nm.]

Q: The shortest wavelength in the Balmer series is:
A) 364.6 nm
B) 656.3 nm
C) 121.6 nm
D) 91.2 nm
Answer: A [n1=2, n2=∞ gives lambda = 4/R = 364.6 nm.]

Q: The wavelength of the first line of the Lyman series is 121.6 nm. The wavelength of the second line is:
A) 102.6 nm
B) 91.2 nm
C) 656.3 nm
D) 1875 nm
Answer: A [First line: n1=1, n2=2 -> 121.6 nm. Second line: n1=1, n2=3 -> lambda = 1/(R(1 - 1/9)) = 1/(R*8/9) = 9/(8R) = 102.6 nm.]

Q: The ratio of the radii of the first and second Bohr orbits is:
A) 1:4
B) 1:2
C) 1:8
D) 1:16
Answer: A [r_n ∝ n^2. So r1:r2 = 1:4.]

Q: The ratio of the velocities of the electron in the first and second Bohr orbits is:
A) 2:1
B) 1:2
C) 1:4
D) 4:1
Answer: A [v_n ∝ 1/n. So v1:v2 = 2:1.]

Q: The ratio of the energies of the first and second Bohr orbits is:
A) 4:1
B) 1:4
C) 2:1
D) 1:2
Answer: A [E_n ∝ 1/n^2. So E1:E2 = 4:1.]

Q: The energy required to excite an electron from the first to the second orbit of hydrogen is:
A) 10.2 eV
B) 13.6 eV
C) 3.4 eV
D) 1.51 eV
Answer: A [E = E2 - E1 = -3.4 - (-13.6) = 10.2 eV.]

Q: The energy released when an electron jumps from the second to the first orbit is:
A) 10.2 eV
B) 13.6 eV
C) 3.4 eV
D) 1.51 eV
Answer: A [Delta_E = E2 - E1 = -3.4 - (-13.6) = 10.2 eV.]

Q: The energy of the photon emitted when an electron jumps from n=3 to n=2 is:
A) 1.89 eV
B) 10.2 eV
C) 12.09 eV
D) 2.55 eV
Answer: A [E = E3 - E2 = -1.51 - (-3.4) = 1.89 eV.]

Q: The wavelength of the photon emitted in the above transition is:
A) 656.3 nm
B) 121.6 nm
C) 102.6 nm
D) 91.2 nm
Answer: A [lambda = hc / E = 1240 / 1.89 = 656.3 nm.]

Q: The number of spectral lines emitted when an electron falls from n=4 to n=1 is:
A) 6
B) 4
C) 3
D) 5
Answer: A [n(n-1)/2 = 4*3/2 = 6 lines.]

Q: The radius of the second Bohr orbit of hydrogen is:
A) 2.116 A
B) 0.529 A
C) 1.058 A
D) 4.232 A
Answer: A [r2 = 0.529 * 4 = 2.116 A.]

Q: The velocity of the electron in the third Bohr orbit is:
A) 7.27 × 10^5 m/s
B) 2.18 × 10^6 m/s
C) 1.09 × 10^6 m/s
D) 5.45 × 10^5 m/s
Answer: A [v3 = 2.18e6 / 3 = 7.27e5 m/s.]

Q: The energy of the fourth orbit of hydrogen is:
A) -0.85 eV
B) -1.51 eV
C) -3.4 eV
D) -13.6 eV
Answer: A [E4 = -13.6 / 16 = -0.85 eV.]

Q: The wavelength of the photon required to ionize a hydrogen atom from the ground state is:
A) 91.2 nm
B) 121.6 nm
C) 656.3 nm
D) 102.6 nm
Answer: A [Ionization from n=1 to n=∞ gives lambda = 1/R = 91.2 nm.]

Q: The energy of the photon that can excite an electron from n=2 to n=4 is:
A) 2.55 eV
B) 1.89 eV
C) 10.2 eV
D) 12.09 eV
Answer: A [E = E4 - E2 = -0.85 - (-3.4) = 2.55 eV.]

Q: The angular momentum of an electron in the third Bohr orbit is:
A) 3h / 2pi
B) h / 2pi
C) 2h / 2pi
D) 4h / 2pi
Answer: A [L = n h / 2pi = 3h / 2pi.]

Q: The ratio of the frequencies of the first lines of the Lyman and Balmer series is:
A) 27:5
B) 5:27
C) 3:1
D) 1:3
Answer: A [f_Lyman = c / 121.6 nm, f_Balmer = c / 656.3 nm. Ratio = 656.3 / 121.6 = 5.4 ≈ 27:5.]

Q: The radius of the first Bohr orbit of hydrogen is 0.529 A. The radius of the first orbit of He+ is:
A) 0.2645 A
B) 0.529 A
C) 1.058 A
D) 2.116 A
Answer: A [For hydrogen-like atoms, r_n ∝ n^2 / Z. For He+ (Z=2), r1 = 0.529 / 2 = 0.2645 A.]

Q: The ionization energy of He+ is:
A) 54.4 eV
B) 13.6 eV
C) 27.2 eV
D) 108.8 eV
Answer: A [Ionization energy ∝ Z^2. For He+ (Z=2), IE = 13.6 * 4 = 54.4 eV.]

Q: The Bohr model is applicable to:
A) Hydrogen-like atoms
B) All atoms
C) Molecules
D) Solids
Answer: A [Bohr model works for single-electron systems (H, He+, Li2+, etc.).]

Q: The limit of the Lyman series is:
A) 91.2 nm
B) 121.6 nm
C) 656.3 nm
D) 364.6 nm
Answer: A [Lyman limit: n1=1, n2=∞ -> 91.2 nm.]

Q: The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom is called:
A) Atomic number (Z)
B) Mass number (A)
C) Neutron number (N)
D) Nucleon number
Answer: A [Atomic number Z is the number of protons.]
