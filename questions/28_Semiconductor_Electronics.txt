# 28_Semiconductor_Electronics

Q: A semiconductor has resistivity:
A) Between a conductor and an insulator
B) Less than a conductor
C) Greater than an insulator
D) Zero
Answer: A [Semiconductors have resistivity between conductors and insulators.]

Q: The most commonly used semiconductor materials are:
A) Silicon and Germanium
B) Copper and Aluminum
C) Silver and Gold
D) Carbon and Diamond
Answer: A [Silicon and Germanium are the most common semiconductors.]

Q: At absolute zero temperature, a semiconductor behaves as:
A) An insulator
B) A conductor
C) A superconductor
D) A semiconductor
Answer: A [At 0 K, no free electrons, so it behaves as an insulator.]

Q: The energy gap of a semiconductor is of the order of:
A) 1 eV
B) 0.1 eV
C) 10 eV
D) 100 eV
Answer: A [Eg ≈ 1 eV (Si: 1.1 eV, Ge: 0.7 eV).]

Q: The energy gap of an insulator is:
A) Greater than 3 eV
B) Less than 1 eV
C) About 1 eV
D) Zero
Answer: A [Insulators have Eg > 3 eV.]

Q: The energy gap of a conductor is:
A) Zero
B) Small
C) Large
D) Infinite
Answer: A [Conductors have overlapping bands, so Eg = 0.]

Q: In an intrinsic semiconductor, the number of electrons and holes is:
A) Equal
B) Unequal
C) Zero
D) Infinite
Answer: A [In intrinsic semiconductors, n = p.]

Q: The charge carriers in an intrinsic semiconductor are:
A) Electrons and holes
B) Only electrons
C) Only holes
D) Ions
Answer: A [Both electrons and holes are charge carriers.]

Q: The conductivity of an intrinsic semiconductor increases with:
A) Increase in temperature
B) Decrease in temperature
C) Increase in pressure
D) Decrease in pressure
Answer: A [More electron-hole pairs are generated at higher temperatures.]

Q: An extrinsic semiconductor is formed by:
A) Doping an intrinsic semiconductor
B) Heating a semiconductor
C) Cooling a semiconductor
D) Compressing a semiconductor
Answer: A [Doping adds impurities to create extrinsic semiconductors.]

Q: The dopants used in n-type semiconductors are:
A) Pentavalent elements (e.g., P, As, Sb)
B) Trivalent elements (e.g., B, Al, Ga)
C) Tetravalent elements (e.g., Si, Ge)
D) Divalent elements
Answer: A [Pentavalent dopants donate electrons, creating n-type.]

Q: The dopants used in p-type semiconductors are:
A) Trivalent elements (e.g., B, Al, Ga)
B) Pentavalent elements (e.g., P, As, Sb)
C) Tetravalent elements (e.g., Si, Ge)
D) Divalent elements
Answer: A [Trivalent dopants accept electrons, creating holes (p-type).]

Q: In an n-type semiconductor, the majority charge carriers are:
A) Electrons
B) Holes
C) Both electrons and holes
D) Ions
Answer: A [Electrons are majority carriers in n-type.]

Q: In a p-type semiconductor, the majority charge carriers are:
A) Holes
B) Electrons
C) Both electrons and holes
D) Ions
Answer: A [Holes are majority carriers in p-type.]

Q: The Fermi level in an n-type semiconductor is:
A) Close to the conduction band
B) Close to the valence band
C) In the middle of the energy gap
D) Above the conduction band
Answer: A [Fermi level shifts towards the conduction band in n-type.]

Q: The Fermi level in a p-type semiconductor is:
A) Close to the valence band
B) Close to the conduction band
C) In the middle of the energy gap
D) Above the conduction band
Answer: A [Fermi level shifts towards the valence band in p-type.]

Q: In a p-n junction diode, the depletion region is formed:
A) At the junction
B) In the p-region only
C) In the n-region only
D) Outside the diode
Answer: A [The depletion region forms at the junction of p and n regions.]

Q: The depletion region contains:
A) Immobile ionized dopants
B) Mobile charge carriers
C) Free electrons
D) Free holes
Answer: A [The depletion region has fixed ionic charges (no mobile carriers).]

Q: The barrier potential in a silicon p-n junction diode is approximately:
A) 0.7 V
B) 0.3 V
C) 1.1 V
D) 0.2 V
Answer: A [Silicon diode barrier potential ≈ 0.7 V.]

Q: The barrier potential in a germanium p-n junction diode is approximately:
A) 0.3 V
B) 0.7 V
C) 1.1 V
D) 0.2 V
Answer: A [Germanium diode barrier potential ≈ 0.3 V.]

Q: In forward bias, the p-n junction diode:
A) Conducts current
B) Blocks current
C) Acts as an insulator
D) Breaks down
Answer: A [In forward bias, diode conducts.]

Q: In reverse bias, the p-n junction diode:
A) Blocks current
B) Conducts current
C) Acts as a conductor
D) Emits light
Answer: A [In reverse bias, diode blocks current (except leakage).]

Q: The breakdown voltage of a diode is:
A) The reverse voltage at which it conducts heavily
B) The forward voltage at which it conducts
C) The voltage at which it emits light
D) The voltage at which it stores charge
Answer: A [Breakdown voltage is the reverse voltage at which diode conducts heavily.]

Q: A Zener diode is used for:
A) Voltage regulation
B) Rectification
C) Amplification
D) Oscillation
Answer: A [Zener diode is used as a voltage regulator.]

Q: A light-emitting diode (LED) emits light when:
A) Forward biased
B) Reverse biased
C) Unbiased
D) At breakdown
Answer: A [LED emits light in forward bias.]

Q: A photodiode works in:
A) Reverse bias
B) Forward bias
C) Unbiased
D) Zero bias
Answer: A [Photodiodes are operated in reverse bias.]

Q: A solar cell converts:
A) Light energy into electrical energy
B) Electrical energy into light energy
C) Heat energy into electrical energy
D) Electrical energy into heat energy
Answer: A [Solar cells convert solar energy into electrical energy.]

Q: The transistor is a:
A) Three-terminal semiconductor device
B) Two-terminal device
C) Four-terminal device
D) One-terminal device
Answer: A [Transistor has three terminals: emitter, base, and collector.]

Q: The three terminals of a bipolar junction transistor (BJT) are:
A) Emitter, base, and collector
B) Anode, cathode, and gate
C) Source, drain, and gate
D) Emitter, collector, and gate
Answer: A [BJT terminals: Emitter (E), Base (B), Collector (C).]

Q: In a BJT, the emitter region is:
A) Heavily doped
B) Lightly doped
C) Moderately doped
D) Undoped
Answer: A [Emitter is heavily doped.]

Q: In a BJT, the base region is:
A) Thin and lightly doped
B) Thick and heavily doped
C) Thin and heavily doped
D) Thick and lightly doped
Answer: A [Base is thin and lightly doped.]

Q: In a BJT, the collector region is:
A) Moderately doped and larger
B) Heavily doped
C) Lightly doped
D) Thin and lightly doped
Answer: A [Collector is moderately doped and larger in size.]

Q: In an n-p-n transistor, the majority carriers in the emitter are:
A) Electrons
B) Holes
C) Both electrons and holes
D) Ions
Answer: A [In n-p-n, emitter is n-type, so electrons are majority carriers.]

Q: In a p-n-p transistor, the majority carriers in the emitter are:
A) Holes
B) Electrons
C) Both electrons and holes
D) Ions
Answer: A [In p-n-p, emitter is p-type, so holes are majority carriers.]

Q: The current gain of a transistor is:
A) beta = I_c / I_b
B) beta = I_b / I_c
C) beta = I_c / I_e
D) beta = I_e / I_c
Answer: A [Current gain beta = I_c / I_b.]

Q: The relation between alpha and beta of a transistor is:
A) beta = alpha / (1 - alpha)
B) alpha = beta / (1 - beta)
C) beta = alpha / (1 + alpha)
D) alpha = beta / (1 + beta)
Answer: A [alpha = I_c / I_e. beta = alpha / (1 - alpha).]

Q: In the common-emitter configuration, the transistor is used for:
A) Amplification
B) Rectification
C) Voltage regulation
D) Oscillation
Answer: A [Common-emitter configuration is used for amplification.]

Q: The logic gate that gives output 1 only when all inputs are 1 is:
A) AND gate
B) OR gate
C) NOT gate
D) NAND gate
Answer: A [AND gate output is 1 only when all inputs are 1.]

Q: The logic gate that gives output 1 when at least one input is 1 is:
A) OR gate
B) AND gate
C) NOT gate
D) NOR gate
Answer: A [OR gate output is 1 if any input is 1.]

Q: The logic gate that inverts the input is:
A) NOT gate
B) AND gate
C) OR gate
D) NAND gate
Answer: A [NOT gate inverts the input (0 → 1, 1 → 0).]

Q: The truth table of a NAND gate is the complement of:
A) AND gate
B) OR gate
C) NOT gate
D) NOR gate
Answer: A [NAND is the complement of AND.]

Q: The truth table of a NOR gate is the complement of:
A) OR gate
B) AND gate
C) NOT gate
D) NAND gate
Answer: A [NOR is the complement of OR.]

Q: The number of inputs in a basic logic gate is:
A) Two for AND/OR, one for NOT
B) Two for all gates
C) One for all gates
D) Three for all gates
Answer: A [AND/OR gates have 2 inputs, NOT gate has 1 input.]

Q: A digital circuit that performs the OR operation followed by NOT is:
A) NOR gate
B) NAND gate
C) AND gate
D) XOR gate
Answer: A [NOR = OR + NOT.]

Q: A digital circuit that performs the AND operation followed by NOT is:
A) NAND gate
B) NOR gate
C) OR gate
D) XOR gate
Answer: A [NAND = AND + NOT.]

Q: The XOR gate gives output 1 when:
A) The number of 1 inputs is odd
B) All inputs are 1
C) All inputs are 0
D) At least one input is 1
Answer: A [XOR output is 1 for odd number of 1 inputs.]

Q: The logic gate used as a universal gate is:
A) NAND and NOR
B) AND and OR
C) NOT and AND
D) OR and NOT
Answer: A [NAND and NOR are universal gates (can build any other gate).]

Q: The boolean expression for the AND gate is:
A) Y = A . B
B) Y = A + B
C) Y = A . B (bar)
D) Y = A + B (bar)
Answer: A [AND: Y = A AND B = A . B.]

Q: The boolean expression for the OR gate is:
A) Y = A + B
B) Y = A . B
C) Y = A . B (bar)
D) Y = A + B (bar)
Answer: A [OR: Y = A OR B = A + B.]

Q: The boolean expression for the NOT gate is:
A) Y = A (bar)
B) Y = A
C) Y = A . B
D) Y = A + B
Answer: A [NOT: Y = NOT A = A̅ (A bar).]

Q: The boolean expression for the NAND gate is:
A) Y = A . B (bar)
B) Y = A + B
C) Y = A . B
D) Y = A + B (bar)
Answer: A [NAND: Y = (A . B) bar = A̅ + B̅.]

Q: The boolean expression for the NOR gate is:
A) Y = A + B (bar)
B) Y = A . B
C) Y = A + B
D) Y = A . B (bar)
Answer: A [NOR: Y = (A + B) bar = A̅ . B̅.]

Q: The logic family with the highest speed is:
A) ECL (Emitter Coupled Logic)
B) TTL (Transistor-Transistor Logic)
C) CMOS (Complementary MOS)
D) RTL (Resistor-Transistor Logic)
Answer: A [ECL is the fastest logic family.]

Q: The logic family with the lowest power consumption is:
A) CMOS
B) TTL
C) ECL
D) RTL
Answer: A [CMOS has very low power consumption.]

Q: In a transistor, the current gain beta is typically:
A) Between 20 and 200
B) Less than 1
C) Greater than 1000
D) Zero
Answer: A [Beta (β) is typically 20 to 200.]

Q: The input impedance of a common-emitter amplifier is:
A) Low
B) High
C) Infinite
D) Zero
Answer: A [Common-emitter input impedance is low (~1 kΩ).]

Q: The output impedance of a common-emitter amplifier is:
A) High
B) Low
C) Zero
D) Infinite
Answer: A [Common-emitter output impedance is high.]
