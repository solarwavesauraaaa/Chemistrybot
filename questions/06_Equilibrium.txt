Q: Which of the following is a correct characteristic of a system at dynamic equilibrium for a physical process?
A) The opposing processes stop completely
B) Measurable properties remain constant while both forward and reverse processes continue at equal rates
C) Equilibrium can be attained in an open system
D) The equilibrium vapour pressure depends on the amount of liquid present
Answer: B [Explanation: At equilibrium both opposing processes occur simultaneously at equal rates so that macroscopic properties remain constant; the equilibrium is dynamic and possible only in a closed system.]

Q: For the equilibrium H₂O(l) ⇌ H₂O(g) in a closed vessel at a fixed temperature, the equilibrium vapour pressure
A) increases with increase in the volume of the vessel
B) decreases if some more liquid water is added
C) remains constant irrespective of the amount of liquid or vapour present (provided both phases coexist)
D) becomes zero when the liquid completely evaporates
Answer: C [Explanation: Equilibrium vapour pressure is constant at a given temperature as long as liquid and vapour coexist; it is independent of the absolute amounts of the two phases.]

Q: When solid iodine is placed in a closed vessel, the intensity of violet vapour becomes constant after some time. This indicates
A) all solid iodine has sublimed
B) the rate of sublimation has become zero
C) a dynamic equilibrium I₂(s) ⇌ I₂(g) has been established
D) the system has become open
Answer: C [Explanation: Constancy of colour intensity shows that the rate of sublimation equals the rate of condensation, establishing dynamic equilibrium.]

Q: Henry's law governs the equilibrium
A) Sugar(s) ⇌ Sugar(aq)
B) CO₂(g) ⇌ CO₂(aq)
C) H₂O(s) ⇌ H₂O(l)
D) I₂(s) ⇌ I₂(g)
Answer: B [Explanation: Henry's law states that the mass of a gas dissolved in a given mass of solvent is proportional to the pressure of the gas above the solvent at constant temperature.]

Q: In the radioactive-tracer experiment with sugar, radioactivity appears in both the solid and the solution phases of a saturated solution. This proves
A) the equilibrium is static
B) the rate of dissolution equals the rate of crystallisation (dynamic equilibrium)
C) sugar does not dissolve in water
D) the solubility of sugar is infinite
Answer: B [Explanation: Exchange of radioactive and non-radioactive molecules between solid and solution demonstrates continuous dissolution and crystallisation at equal rates.]

Q: For the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), the use of deuterium (D₂) in place of H₂ and subsequent mixing of the two equilibrium mixtures leads to formation of mixed isotopologues (NH₂D, NHD₂, etc.). This observation confirms
A) the reaction stops completely at equilibrium
B) the equilibrium is dynamic (forward and reverse reactions continue)
C) ammonia cannot be formed from nitrogen and hydrogen
D) the equilibrium constant changes with isotopes
Answer: B [Explanation: Scrambling of H and D atoms can occur only if both forward and reverse reactions continue after the macroscopic concentrations have become constant.]

Q: The equilibrium constant expression for H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g) is
A) Kc = [H₂][I₂]/[HI]²
B) Kc = [HI]²/[H₂][I₂]
C) Kc = [HI]/[H₂][I₂]
D) Kc = [HI]² - [H₂] - [I₂]
Answer: B [Explanation: By the law of mass action, Kc is the product of the concentrations of products raised to their stoichiometric coefficients divided by the corresponding product for reactants.]

Q: From the experimental data for the H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI equilibrium at 731 K, the value of Kc is found to be the same whether one starts with pure reactants or pure product. This shows that
A) equilibrium can be approached from either direction
B) Kc depends on the starting materials
C) the reaction is irreversible
D) the equilibrium constant is zero
Answer: A [Explanation: The same numerical value of Kc is obtained from both directions, proving that equilibrium is independent of the direction of approach.]

Q: For a general reaction aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, the correct relationship between Kp and Kc is
A) Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn where Δn = (c+d) - (a+b)
B) Kp = Kc(RT)^(-Δn)
C) Kp = Kc always
D) Kp = KcRT
Answer: A [Explanation: From the ideal-gas law, partial pressures pi = ciRT; substitution into the Kp expression yields Kp = Kc(RT)^Δng.]

Q: For the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), Δng =
A) +2
B) -2
C) 0
D) +1
Answer: B [Explanation: Δng = 2 - (1+3) = -2.]

Q: Which of the following statements about the equilibrium constant is false?
A) Kc is independent of the initial concentrations of reactants and products
B) Kc changes with temperature
C) A large value of Kc means the equilibrium lies far toward products
D) Kc depends on the presence of a catalyst
Answer: D [Explanation: A catalyst accelerates both forward and reverse rates equally and does not alter the position of equilibrium or the value of Kc.]

Q: At equilibrium for a reversible reaction, the free-energy change ΔG is
A) positive
B) negative
C) zero
D) equal to ΔG°
Answer: C [Explanation: At equilibrium ΔG = 0 (the system is at minimum Gibbs energy).]

Q: According to Le Chatelier's principle, addition of an inert gas at constant volume to the equilibrium N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃
A) shifts the equilibrium to the right
B) shifts the equilibrium to the left
C) has no effect on the equilibrium position
D) increases Kc
Answer: C [Explanation: At constant volume the partial pressures (and therefore concentrations) of the reacting species remain unchanged; an inert gas does not affect the equilibrium position.]

Q: For the endothermic reaction PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g), an increase in temperature
A) decreases Kc
B) increases Kc
C) leaves Kc unchanged
D) shifts the equilibrium to the left
Answer: B [Explanation: For an endothermic reaction heat may be regarded as a reactant; raising temperature favours the forward reaction and therefore increases Kc.]

Q: The degree of dissociation of PCl₅ increases with
A) increase in pressure
B) decrease in volume at constant temperature
C) addition of Cl₂ at constant volume
D) decrease in pressure (or increase in volume)
Answer: D [Explanation: The reaction proceeds with an increase in the number of moles; lowering pressure favours the side with more moles, increasing the degree of dissociation.]

Q: Which of the following is a Brønsted–Lowry acid but not an Arrhenius acid?
A) HCl in water
B) NH₄⁺
C) NaOH
D) Ca(OH)₂
Answer: B [Explanation: NH₄⁺ can donate a proton (NH₄⁺ ⇌ NH₃ + H⁺) and is therefore a Brønsted acid, but it does not produce H⁺ by dissociation in the Arrhenius sense.]

Q: According to the Lewis concept, BF₃ is an acid because it
A) donates a proton
B) accepts a proton
C) accepts an electron pair
D) donates an electron pair
Answer: C [Explanation: Lewis acids are electron-pair acceptors; the vacant orbital on boron allows BF₃ to accept a lone pair.]

Q: The conjugate base of H₂PO₄⁻ is
A) H₃PO₄
B) HPO₄²⁻
C) PO₄³⁻
D) H₂PO₄⁻ itself
Answer: B [Explanation: Removal of one proton from H₂PO₄⁻ yields HPO₄²⁻.]

Q: For a weak acid HA with dissociation constant Ka, the degree of dissociation α at concentration C (assuming α << 1) is approximately
A) √(Ka/C)
B) Ka/C
C) √(KaC)
D) KaC
Answer: A [Explanation: Ka = Cα²/(1-α) ≈ Cα² ⇒ α ≈ √(Ka/C).]

Q: The pH of a 10⁻⁸ M HCl solution is approximately
A) 8
B) 6.98
C) 7
D) 1
Answer: B [Explanation: Contribution of H⁺ from water must be taken into account; solving the quadratic gives [H⁺] ≈ 1.05 × 10⁻⁷ M, so pH ≈ 6.98.]

Q: The ionic product of water Kw at 25 °C is 10⁻¹⁴. At this temperature the pH of pure water is
A) 0
B) 7
C) 14
D) undefined
Answer: B [Explanation: [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = 10⁻⁷ M ⇒ pH = 7.]

Q: A buffer solution is prepared by mixing equal volumes of 0.1 M CH₃COOH (Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) and 0.1 M CH₃COONa. The pH of the buffer is approximately
A) 4.74
B) 7
C) 9.26
D) 2.87
Answer: A [Explanation: Henderson–Hasselbalch: pH = pKa + log([salt]/[acid]) = 4.74 + log1 = 4.74.]

Q: Addition of a common ion to a weak electrolyte
A) increases its degree of dissociation
B) decreases its degree of dissociation
C) has no effect on dissociation
D) increases Ka
Answer: B [Explanation: Common-ion effect suppresses the dissociation of the weak electrolyte (Le Chatelier's principle).]

Q: The solubility product of AgCl is 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰. The solubility of AgCl in pure water is
A) 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ mol L⁻¹
B) 1.34 × 10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹
C) 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ mol L⁻¹
D) 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹
Answer: B [Explanation: Ksp = s² ⇒ s = √(1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰) = 1.34 × 10⁻⁵ mol L⁻¹.]

Q: The solubility of AgCl is least in
A) pure water
B) 0.1 M HBr
C) 0.1 M AgNO₃
D) 0.1 M NaNO₃
Answer: C [Explanation: Presence of a common ion (Cl⁻ or Ag⁺) suppresses solubility by the common-ion effect; NaNO₃ has no common ion.]

Q: For the equilibrium CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g), Kp is equal to
A) pCO₂
B) pCaO
C) pCaCO₃
D) 1/pCO₂
Answer: A [Explanation: Pure solids do not appear in the equilibrium-constant expression; Kp = pCO₂.]

Q: Which of the following does not affect the value of the equilibrium constant Kc?
A) Temperature
B) Catalyst
C) Nature of reactants
D) Stoichiometry of the reaction
Answer: B [Explanation: A catalyst changes the rate but not the position of equilibrium or the value of Kc.]

Q: In the reaction 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), an increase in pressure at constant temperature
A) decreases the yield of SO₃
B) increases the yield of SO₃
C) has no effect on the yield
D) decreases Kc
Answer: B [Explanation: The forward reaction proceeds with a decrease in the number of moles; higher pressure favours the side with fewer moles.]

Q: The pH of a solution obtained by mixing 50 mL of 0.2 M HCl with 50 mL of 0.2 M NaOH is
A) 0
B) 7
C) 1
D) 13
Answer: B [Explanation: Exact neutralisation of a strong acid by a strong base yields a neutral solution of NaCl; pH = 7 (at 25 °C).]

Q: Which of the following is a Lewis base?
A) BF₃
B) AlCl₃
C) NH₃
D) H⁺
Answer: C [Explanation: NH₃ possesses a lone pair and can donate it; it is a Lewis base.]

Q: The relationship between Kp and Kc for the reaction PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g) is
A) Kp = Kc
B) Kp = KcRT
C) Kp = Kc/RT
D) Kp = Kc(RT)²
Answer: B [Explanation: Δng = +1; therefore Kp = Kc(RT)¹.]

Q: At 25 °C the pH of a 10⁻³ M solution of a weak acid HA (Ka = 10⁻⁵) is approximately
A) 3
B) 4
C) 5
D) 2
Answer: B [Explanation: α = √(Ka/C) = √(10⁻²) = 0.1; [H⁺] = Cα = 10⁻⁴ M ⇒ pH = 4.]

Q: A solution of sodium acetate in water is
A) acidic because of hydrolysis of acetate ion
B) basic because of hydrolysis of acetate ion
C) neutral
D) acidic because of hydrolysis of sodium ion
Answer: B [Explanation: CH₃COO⁻ hydrolyses: CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + OH⁻, producing a basic solution.]

Q: The unit of Kc for the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) is
A) mol² L⁻²
B) dimensionless
C) mol L⁻¹
D) L² mol⁻²
Answer: D [Explanation: Kc = [NH₃]²/[N₂][H₂]³ has units of (concentration)⁻² = L² mol⁻².]

Q: Which of the following equilibria is not affected by a change in pressure?
A) H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)
B) N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)
C) PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g)
D) 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)
Answer: A [Explanation: Δng = 0; change in pressure does not shift the equilibrium position.]

Q: The conjugate acid of HPO₄²⁻ is
A) H₂PO₄⁻
B) PO₄³⁻
C) H₃PO₄
D) HPO₄²⁻ itself
Answer: A [Explanation: Addition of a proton to HPO₄²⁻ gives H₂PO₄⁻.]

Q: For a sparingly soluble salt A₂B, Ksp = 4s³ where s is the molar solubility. This salt is of the type
A) AB
B) AB₂
C) A₂B
D) A₃B
Answer: C [Explanation: A₂B(s) ⇌ 2A⁺ + B²⁻; Ksp = (2s)² × s = 4s³.]

Q: The pH of a buffer solution containing 0.1 M NH₄OH (Kb = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵) and 0.1 M NH₄Cl is approximately
A) 4.74
B) 9.26
C) 7
D) 10.26
Answer: B [Explanation: For a basic buffer pOH = pKb + log([salt]/[base]) = 4.74; pH = 14 - 4.74 = 9.26.]

Q: When a catalyst is added to a reversible reaction at equilibrium
A) the value of Kc increases
B) the equilibrium shifts to the right
C) the rates of both forward and reverse reactions increase equally
D) the equilibrium is disturbed
Answer: C [Explanation: A catalyst lowers the activation energy for both directions equally; equilibrium composition remains unchanged.]

Q: The equilibrium constant for the reaction 2A ⇌ B + C is 2 × 10⁻³. At a given temperature, if the concentration of A at equilibrium is 0.1 M, the concentration of B is
A) 2 × 10⁻⁴ M
B) 2 × 10⁻³ M
C) 4.47 × 10⁻³ M
D) 1 × 10⁻³ M
Answer: C [Explanation: Kc = [B][C]/[A]² = 2 × 10⁻³. Assuming [B] = [C] = x, x²/(0.1)² = 2 × 10⁻³ ⇒ x = √(2 × 10⁻⁵) = 4.47 × 10⁻³ M.]

Q: Which of the following is true for a saturated solution of a sparingly soluble salt?
A) Ksp > ionic product
B) Ksp = ionic product
C) Ksp < ionic product
D) Ksp is zero
Answer: B [Explanation: At saturation the solution is in equilibrium with the solid; ionic product equals Ksp.]

Q: The degree of ionisation of a weak electrolyte is inversely proportional to the square root of
A) dilution
B) concentration
C) temperature
D) pressure
Answer: B [Explanation: α = √(K/C); hence α ∝ 1/√C.]

Q: For the reaction H₂(g) + CO₂(g) ⇌ H₂O(g) + CO(g), Kc = 0.64 at 900 K. Starting with 1 mol each of H₂ and CO₂ in a 1 L vessel, the number of moles of CO at equilibrium is
A) 0.4
B) 0.8
C) 0.2
D) 0.6
Answer: A [Explanation: Let x be the extent of reaction. Kc = x²/(1-x)² = 0.64 ⇒ x/(1-x) = 0.8 ⇒ x = 0.44 ≈ 0.4 (nearest option).]

Q: An aqueous solution of FeCl₃ is acidic because of
A) hydrolysis of Fe³⁺
B) hydrolysis of Cl⁻
C) oxidation of Fe³⁺
D) reduction of Cl⁻
Answer: A [Explanation: Fe³⁺ hydrolyses: Fe³⁺ + H₂O ⇌ Fe(OH)²⁺ + H⁺, producing H⁺.]

Q: The correct order of acidic strength is   
A) HClO₄ > HClO₂ > HClO₃ > HClO
B) HClO > HClO₂ > HClO₃ > HClO₄
C) HClO₂ > HClO > HClO₃ > HClO₄
D) HClO₄ > HClO₃ > HClO₂ > HClO
Answer: D [Explanation: Acidic strength of oxoacids of chlorine increases with the number of oxygen atoms attached to the central atom (increasing oxidation state stabilises the conjugate base).]
