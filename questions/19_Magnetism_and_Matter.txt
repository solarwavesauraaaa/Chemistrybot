# 19_Magnetism_and_Matter

Q: The magnetic field lines due to a bar magnet:
A) Emerge from the north pole and enter the south pole
B) Emerge from the south pole and enter the north pole
C) Are parallel to the axis
D) Are perpendicular to the axis
Answer: A [Field lines go from N to S outside the magnet.]

Q: A freely suspended bar magnet aligns itself:
A) Along the north-south direction
B) Along the east-west direction
C) Randomly
D) Perpendicular to the earth's field
Answer: A [A magnet aligns along the Earth's magnetic NS direction.]

Q: The pole strength of a magnet is a measure of:
A) The strength of its magnetic poles
B) The length of the magnet
C) The area of the magnet
D) The volume of the magnet
Answer: A [Pole strength (m) determines magnetic dipole moment.]

Q: The magnetic dipole moment of a bar magnet is:
A) m = pole strength × magnetic length
B) m = pole strength / length
C) m = length / pole strength
D) m = pole strength + length
Answer: A [M = m × 2l.]

Q: The SI unit of magnetic dipole moment is:
A) A m^2
B) T
C) N/A m
D) Wb
Answer: A [Magnetic moment unit = Ampere meter^2.]

Q: The magnetic field at a point on the axis of a bar magnet is:
A) B = (mu0 / 4pi) 2M / r^3
B) B = (mu0 / 4pi) M / r^3
C) B = (mu0 / 4pi) 2M / r^2
D) B = (mu0 / 4pi) M / r^2
Answer: A [B_axis = (mu0/4pi) 2M / r^3.]

Q: The magnetic field at a point on the equatorial line of a bar magnet is:
A) B = (mu0 / 4pi) M / r^3
B) B = (mu0 / 4pi) 2M / r^3
C) B = (mu0 / 4pi) M / r^2
D) B = (mu0 / 4pi) 2M / r^2
Answer: A [B_equatorial = (mu0/4pi) M / r^3.]

Q: The magnetic field at a point due to a bar magnet is:
A) Axial field > Equatorial field
B) Axial field < Equatorial field
C) Axial field = Equatorial field
D) Zero
Answer: A [B_axis = 2B_equatorial for same r.]

Q: A magnet is cut into two equal halves. The magnetic moment of each half:
A) Becomes half
B) Doubles
C) Remains same
D) Becomes zero
Answer: A [If cut perpendicular to axis, M becomes half.]

Q: A magnet is cut into two equal halves along its length. The magnetic moment of each half:
A) Remains same
B) Becomes half
C) Doubles
D) Becomes zero
Answer: A [If cut along length, pole strength remains same, length remains same, so M remains same.]

Q: The magnetic field due to a bar magnet at a point on its axis is B. If the distance is doubled, the field becomes:
A) B/8
B) B/4
C) B/2
D) 2B
Answer: A [B ∝ 1/r^3. So B' = B/8.]

Q: The magnetic field due to a bar magnet at a point on its equatorial line is B. If the distance is doubled, the field becomes:
A) B/8
B) B/4
C) B/2
D) 2B
Answer: A [B ∝ 1/r^3. So B' = B/8.]

Q: The Earth's magnetic field is due to:
A) Electric currents in the core
B) Permanent magnets in the core
C) Solar radiation
D) Moon's gravity
Answer: A [Earth's magnetic field is generated by convection currents in its molten core.]

Q: The magnetic declination is the angle between:
A) Geographic meridian and magnetic meridian
B) Magnetic meridian and horizontal
C) Geographic meridian and horizontal
D) Magnetic axis and geographic axis
Answer: A [Declination is angle between true north and magnetic north.]

Q: The angle of dip (inclination) is the angle between:
A) Magnetic meridian and horizontal plane
B) Magnetic meridian and vertical plane
C) Geographic meridian and horizontal plane
D) Geographic meridian and vertical plane
Answer: A [Dip is angle between B and horizontal.]

Q: At the magnetic equator, the angle of dip is:
A) 0°
B) 90°
C) 45°
D) 60°
Answer: A [At equator, B is horizontal, so dip = 0°.]

Q: At the magnetic poles, the angle of dip is:
A) 90°
B) 0°
C) 45°
D) 60°
Answer: A [At poles, B is vertical, so dip = 90°.]

Q: The horizontal component of Earth's magnetic field is:
A) B_H = B cos(theta)
B) B_H = B sin(theta)
C) B_H = B tan(theta)
D) B_H = B / cos(theta)
Answer: A [B_H = B cos(dip).]

Q: The vertical component of Earth's magnetic field is:
A) B_V = B sin(theta)
B) B_V = B cos(theta)
C) B_V = B tan(theta)
D) B_V = B / sin(theta)
Answer: A [B_V = B sin(dip).]

Q: The magnetic susceptibility (chi) of a material is:
A) The ratio of magnetization to magnetizing field
B) The ratio of magnetic field to magnetization
C) The ratio of permeability to permittivity
D) The magnetic moment per unit volume
Answer: A [chi = M / H.]

Q: The relative permeability (mu_r) of a material is:
A) mu / mu0
B) mu0 / mu
C) mu * mu0
D) mu + mu0
Answer: A [mu_r = mu / mu0.]

Q: The relation between mu_r and chi is:
A) mu_r = 1 + chi
B) mu_r = 1 - chi
C) mu_r = chi / 2
D) mu_r = 2 chi
Answer: A [mu_r = 1 + chi.]

Q: The magnetic permeability of free space (mu0) is:
A) 4pi × 10^-7 H/m
B) 8.854 × 10^-12 H/m
C) 4pi × 10^-7 T m/A
D) Both A and C
Answer: D [mu0 = 4pi × 10^-7 N/A^2 = 4pi × 10^-7 H/m.]

Q: Diamagnetic materials have:
A) Negative susceptibility
B) Positive susceptibility
C) Zero susceptibility
D) Infinite susceptibility
Answer: A [Diamagnets have small negative chi.]

Q: Paramagnetic materials have:
A) Positive susceptibility
B) Negative susceptibility
C) Zero susceptibility
D) Infinite susceptibility
Answer: A [Paramagnets have small positive chi.]

Q: Ferromagnetic materials have:
A) Large positive susceptibility
B) Large negative susceptibility
C) Zero susceptibility
D) Infinite susceptibility
Answer: A [Ferromagnets have very large positive chi.]

Q: In diamagnetic materials, the relative permeability is:
A) Less than 1
B) Greater than 1
C) Equal to 1
D) Negative
Answer: A [mu_r < 1 for diamagnets.]

Q: In paramagnetic materials, the relative permeability is:
A) Greater than 1
B) Less than 1
C) Equal to 1
D) Negative
Answer: A [mu_r > 1 for paramagnets.]

Q: In ferromagnetic materials, the relative permeability is:
A) Much greater than 1
B) Equal to 1
C) Less than 1
D) Zero
Answer: A [mu_r >> 1 for ferromagnets.]

Q: A diamagnetic material is:
A) Repelled by a magnet
B) Attracted by a magnet
C) Not affected by a magnet
D) Magnetized permanently
Answer: A [Diamagnets are weakly repelled.]

Q: A paramagnetic material is:
A) Weakly attracted by a magnet
B) Strongly attracted by a magnet
C) Repelled by a magnet
D) Not affected by a magnet
Answer: A [Paramagnets are weakly attracted.]

Q: A ferromagnetic material is:
A) Strongly attracted by a magnet
B) Weakly attracted by a magnet
C) Repelled by a magnet
D) Not affected by a magnet
Answer: A [Ferromagnets are strongly attracted.]

Q: The Curie temperature is the temperature above which:
A) A ferromagnet becomes paramagnetic
B) A paramagnet becomes ferromagnetic
C) A diamagnet becomes paramagnetic
D) A paramagnet becomes diamagnetic
Answer: A [Above Curie temp, ferromagnets lose magnetic ordering.]

Q: The magnetic moment of a current loop is:
A) m = N I A
B) m = I A
C) m = N A / I
D) m = N I / A
Answer: A [m = N I A.]

Q: A toroid has N turns carrying current I. The magnetic field inside is:
A) B = mu0 N I / 2pi r
B) B = mu0 N I
C) B = mu0 I / 2pi r
D) B = mu0 N I / r
Answer: A [B = mu0 N I / 2pi r.]

Q: A solenoid has N turns per unit length carrying current I. The magnetic field inside is:
A) B = mu0 n I
B) B = mu0 I / n
C) B = mu0 n / I
D) B = mu0 / n I
Answer: A [B = mu0 n I.]

Q: The hysteresis loop is a plot between:
A) B and H
B) M and H
C) B and M
D) B and E
Answer: A [Hysteresis loop: B vs H.]

Q: The area of the hysteresis loop represents:
A) Energy loss per cycle
B) Magnetization
C) Coercivity
D) Retentivity
Answer: A [Area = energy lost per cycle (hysteresis loss).]

Q: Materials with narrow hysteresis loops are used for:
A) Soft magnets
B) Hard magnets
C) Permanent magnets
D) Both B and C
Answer: A [Soft magnets have narrow loops (easy to magnetize/demagnetize).]

Q: Materials with broad hysteresis loops are used for:
A) Permanent magnets
B) Soft magnets
C) Electromagnets
D) Transformer cores
Answer: A [Hard magnets have broad loops (high retentivity).]

Q: The coercivity of a magnetic material is:
A) The reverse field required to reduce B to zero
B) The maximum B it can retain
C) The initial slope of B-H curve
D) The saturation magnetization
Answer: A [Coercivity is the reverse H needed to make B = 0.]

Q: The retentivity of a magnetic material is:
A) The residual magnetism when H is zero
B) The reverse field required to reduce B to zero
C) The initial slope of B-H curve
D) The saturation magnetization
Answer: A [Retentivity is the value of B when H = 0.]

Q: A soft iron core is used in electromagnets because:
A) It has high permeability and low retentivity
B) It has low permeability and high retentivity
C) It has high coercivity
D) It has zero permeability
Answer: A [Soft iron magnetizes easily and loses magnetism quickly.]

Q: A steel core is used in permanent magnets because:
A) It has high retentivity and high coercivity
B) It has low retentivity and high coercivity
C) It has high permeability and low retentivity
D) It has zero retentivity
Answer: A [Steel retains magnetism well.]

Q: The tangent galvanometer works on the principle of:
A) Tangent law
B) Sine law
C) Cosine law
D) Ampere's law
Answer: A [Tangent galvanometer uses tangent law.]

Q: The vibration magnetometer is used to measure:
A) Magnetic moment and horizontal component of B
B) Vertical component of B
C) Declination
D) Dip angle
Answer: A [Vibration magnetometer measures M and B_H.]

Q: The earth's magnetic field at a place is B. The horizontal component is B_H. The angle of dip is:
A) theta = cos^-1(B_H / B)
B) theta = sin^-1(B_H / B)
C) theta = tan^-1(B_H / B)
D) theta = cot^-1(B_H / B)
Answer: A [B_H = B cos(theta).]

Q: The angle of dip at a place is 30°. If B = 5 × 10^-5 T, the horizontal component is:
A) 4.33 × 10^-5 T
B) 2.5 × 10^-5 T
C) 5 × 10^-5 T
D) 8.66 × 10^-5 T
Answer: A [B_H = B cos30° = 5e-5 * 0.866 = 4.33e-5 T.]

Q: The magnetic field at a point on the axis of a bar magnet is B. If the magnet is turned by 180°, the field becomes:
A) -B
B) B
C) 2B
D) B/2
Answer: A [Direction reverses, so B becomes -B.]
