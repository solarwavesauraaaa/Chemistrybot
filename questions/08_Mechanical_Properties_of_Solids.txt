# 08_Mechanical_Properties_of_Solids

Q: The SI unit of stress is:
A) N/m^2
B) N m
C) N/m
D) N/m^3
Answer: A [Stress = Force / Area. SI unit is N/m^2 or Pascal (Pa).]

Q: Which of the following is a dimensionless quantity?
A) Stress
B) Strain
C) Young's modulus
D) Bulk modulus
Answer: B [Strain = change in length / original length. It has no units.]

Q: Hooke's law states that stress is directly proportional to:
A) Strain
B) Force
C) Area
D) Length
Answer: A [Within elastic limit, stress ∝ strain.]

Q: The modulus of elasticity is the ratio of:
A) Stress to strain
B) Strain to stress
C) Force to area
D) Length to area
Answer: A [Modulus of elasticity = Stress / Strain.]

Q: The property of a material to regain its original shape after removing the deforming force is called:
A) Plasticity
B) Elasticity
C) Ductility
D) Malleability
Answer: B [Elasticity is the ability to regain original shape.]

Q: The stress-strain curve for a ductile material shows:
A) A large plastic region
B) No plastic region
C) A straight line only
D) A sharp break
Answer: A [Ductile materials have a large plastic region before fracture.]

Q: The Young's modulus of a wire is Y. If its length is doubled and radius is halved, the Young's modulus will be:
A) Y
B) 2Y
C) Y/2
D) 4Y
Answer: A [Young's modulus is a material property. It does not depend on dimensions.]

Q: A wire of length L and cross-sectional area A is stretched by a force F. The stress is:
A) F / A
B) F * A
C) F / L
D) F * L
Answer: A [Stress = Force / Area = F / A.]

Q: The longitudinal strain is defined as:
A) delta_L / L
B) L / delta_L
C) delta_L * L
D) delta_L / A
Answer: A [Longitudinal strain = (change in length) / (original length).]

Q: The SI unit of Young's modulus is:
A) N/m^2
B) N/m
C) N m
D) N/m^3
Answer: A [Young's modulus has same unit as stress: N/m^2 (Pa).]

Q: The breaking stress of a material is the maximum stress it can withstand before:
A) Fracture
B) Elastic limit
C) Yield point
D) Plastic deformation
Answer: A [Breaking stress is the stress at which material breaks/fractures.]

Q: The elastic limit is the point beyond which the material:
A) Does not return to original shape
B) Returns to original shape
C) Breaks
D) Melts
Answer: A [Beyond elastic limit, permanent deformation occurs.]

Q: The ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain is called:
A) Poisson's ratio
B) Young's modulus
C) Bulk modulus
D) Shear modulus
Answer: A [Poisson's ratio (sigma) = lateral strain / longitudinal strain.]

Q: A wire is stretched by a force F. If the radius of the wire is doubled, the stress becomes:
A) One-fourth
B) Four times
C) Two times
D) Half
Answer: A [Stress = F / A = F / (pi r^2). If r is doubled, area becomes 4 times, stress becomes 1/4.]

Q: The work done in stretching a wire is stored as:
A) Elastic potential energy
B) Kinetic energy
C) Thermal energy
D) Chemical energy
Answer: A [Work done is stored as elastic potential energy in the wire.]

Q: The elastic potential energy per unit volume of a stretched wire is:
A) 0.5 * Stress * Strain
B) Stress * Strain
C) 0.5 * Stress / Strain
D) Stress / Strain
Answer: A [U/V = 0.5 * stress * strain.]

Q: Which of the following materials has the highest Young's modulus?
A) Steel
B) Rubber
C) Copper
D) Aluminum
Answer: A [Steel has a very high Young's modulus (≈ 200 GPa).]

Q: The shear modulus is also known as:
A) Modulus of rigidity
B) Young's modulus
C) Bulk modulus
D) Poisson's ratio
Answer: A [Shear modulus is also called modulus of rigidity (G).]

Q: The bulk modulus of a material is defined as:
A) -V dP / dV
B) dP / dV
C) dV / dP
D) V dP / dV
Answer: A [B = -V (dP/dV).]

Q: The compressibility of a material is the reciprocal of:
A) Bulk modulus
B) Young's modulus
C) Shear modulus
D) Stress
Answer: A [Compressibility = 1 / Bulk modulus.]

Q: A steel wire of length 2 m and cross-sectional area 1 mm^2 is stretched by a force of 200 N. The stress is:
A) 2 x 10^8 N/m^2
B) 2 x 10^6 N/m^2
C) 2 x 10^4 N/m^2
D) 2 x 10^10 N/m^2
Answer: A [Area = 10^-6 m^2. Stress = 200 / 10^-6 = 2 x 10^8 N/m^2.]

Q: The ratio of Young's modulus of steel to that of rubber is:
A) Very large
B) Very small
C) Equal to 1
D) Infinite
Answer: A [Steel is much more rigid than rubber, so ratio is very large.]

Q: The breaking force for a wire is F. If the wire is cut into two equal halves, the breaking force for each half is:
A) F
B) F/2
C) 2F
D) F/4
Answer: A [Breaking force depends on area, not length. So it remains F.]

Q: A wire of length L and radius r is stretched by a force F. The extension is x. If the force is doubled and the radius is halved, the extension becomes:
A) 8x
B) 16x
C) 4x
D) 2x
Answer: A [x = FL / (Y pi r^2). If F becomes 2F and r becomes r/2, new x = (2F)L / (Y pi (r/2)^2) = 2F L / (Y pi r^2/4) = 8x.]

Q: A material has a Young's modulus of 100 GPa. The value in N/m^2 is:
A) 10^11 N/m^2
B) 10^9 N/m^2
C) 10^10 N/m^2
D) 10^8 N/m^2
Answer: A [1 GPa = 10^9 N/m^2. So 100 GPa = 10^11 N/m^2.]

Q: The extension of a wire is x. If its length is doubled and cross-sectional area is halved, the extension for the same force will be:
A) 4x
B) 2x
C) x
D) x/2
Answer: A [x = FL / (Y A). New x = F(2L) / (Y A/2) = 4FL/(YA) = 4x.]

Q: The maximum stress a material can withstand before breaking is called:
A) Ultimate tensile strength
B) Yield strength
C) Elastic limit
D) Breaking stress
Answer: A [Ultimate tensile strength is the maximum stress material can withstand.]

Q: The modulus of elasticity of a perfectly rigid body is:
A) Infinite
B) Zero
C) 1
D) Negative
Answer: A [Rigid body has no strain, so modulus is infinite.]

Q: The strain in a wire is 0.001. If its original length is 2 m, the extension is:
A) 2 mm
B) 0.2 mm
C) 0.02 mm
D) 20 mm
Answer: A [Strain = delta_L / L. delta_L = 0.001 * 2 = 0.002 m = 2 mm.]

Q: A wire of radius r is stretched. If the radius is doubled, the Young's modulus:
A) Remains same
B) Doubles
C) Halves
D) Becomes 4 times
Answer: A [Young's modulus is a material property, independent of dimensions.]

Q: The maximum elongation of a wire before it breaks is called:
A) Ductility
B) Malleability
C) Brittleness
D) Toughness
Answer: A [Ductility is the ability of a material to be drawn into wires.]

Q: The shear strain is defined as the ratio of:
A) Lateral displacement to height
B) Longitudinal displacement to height
C) Change in volume to original volume
D) Change in length to original length
Answer: A [Shear strain = delta_x / h (lateral displacement per unit height).]

Q: The bulk modulus of a liquid is B. If the pressure is increased by P, the fractional change in volume is:
A) P / B
B) B / P
C) P * B
D) 1 / (P B)
Answer: A [B = -P / (delta_V / V). So fractional change = P / B.]

Q: A material has a Poisson's ratio of 0.3. This means:
A) Lateral strain is 0.3 times longitudinal strain
B) Longitudinal strain is 0.3 times lateral strain
C) Stress is 0.3 times strain
D) Strain is 0.3 times stress
Answer: A [Poisson's ratio = lateral strain / longitudinal strain.]

Q: The breaking stress of a material is independent of:
A) Its length
B) Its cross-sectional area
C) Both A and B
D) The force applied
Answer: C [Breaking stress is a material property. It depends on the material, not its dimensions.]

Q: A wire is stretched by a force. The elastic potential energy stored per unit volume is proportional to:
A) Stress^2
B) Strain^2
C) Stress * Strain
D) Force^2
Answer: C [U/V = 0.5 * stress * strain.]

Q: A load of 10 kg is suspended from a wire. The stress in the wire is 2 x 10^7 N/m^2. The cross-sectional area of the wire is (g=10):
A) 5 x 10^-6 m^2
B) 5 x 10^-5 m^2
C) 2 x 10^-6 m^2
D) 10^-5 m^2
Answer: A [Force = mg = 100 N. Stress = F/A -> A = 100 / (2 x 10^7) = 5 x 10^-6 m^2.]

Q: The elongation of a wire is x. If the same force is applied to a wire of same material but double length and double radius, the elongation is:
A) x/2
B) 2x
C) x
D) 4x
Answer: A [x = FL / (Y A). New: x' = F(2L) / (Y * 4A) = 0.5 * x.]

Q: The modulus of rigidity is related to:
A) Shear stress and shear strain
B) Tensile stress and strain
C) Volume stress and strain
D) Thermal stress and strain
Answer: A [Modulus of rigidity G = Shear stress / Shear strain.]

Q: A block of rubber is subjected to a shear stress. The shear strain is proportional to:
A) Shear stress
B) Young's modulus
C) Bulk modulus
D) Poisson's ratio
Answer: A [Within elastic limit, shear strain ∝ shear stress.]

Q: The ductility of a material is measured by:
A) Percentage elongation
B) Percentage reduction in area
C) Both A and B
D) Young's modulus
Answer: C [Ductility is measured by both elongation and area reduction.]

Q: A steel rod and a copper rod of same length and cross-sectional area are subjected to same force. If Y_steel > Y_copper, then:
A) Steel elongates less
B) Copper elongates less
C) Both elongate equally
D) Steel elongates more
Answer: A [Elongation ∝ 1/Y. Higher Y means less elongation.]

Q: A wire has a breaking stress S. If the wire is stretched by a force equal to half the breaking force, the stress is:
A) S/2
B) S
C) 2S
D) S/4
Answer: A [Stress is proportional to force. Force half means stress = S/2.]

Q: A material has Young's modulus Y and Poisson's ratio sigma. The relation between Y, G (shear modulus) and sigma is:
A) Y = 2G(1 + sigma)
B) Y = 2G(1 - sigma)
C) G = 2Y(1 + sigma)
D) G = Y(1 + sigma)
Answer: A [Y = 2G(1 + sigma).]

Q: The strain energy stored in a stretched wire is:
A) 0.5 * Stress * Strain * Volume
B) 0.5 * Stress * Strain
C) Stress * Strain * Volume
D) 0.5 * Stress * Volume
Answer: A [Strain energy = 0.5 * stress * strain * volume.]

Q: The elastic aftereffect is the time delay in:
A) Regaining original shape after removing load
B) Reaching elastic limit
C) Breaking of material
D) Applying load
Answer: A [Elastic aftereffect is the time taken to regain original shape after load is removed.]
