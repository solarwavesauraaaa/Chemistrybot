Q: A solution is prepared by dissolving 18 g glucose in 100 g water at 25°C. The mole fraction of glucose is approximately
A) 0.017
B) 0.034
C) 0.009
D) 0.052
Answer: A [Explanation: n_glucose = 0.1; n_water ≈ 5.556; x = 0.1/5.656 ≈ 0.0177]

Q: Which concentration unit is independent of temperature?
A) Molarity
B) Normality
C) Molality
D) Formality
Answer: C [Explanation: Molality is based on mass of solvent, which does not change with temperature.]

Q: For an ideal binary solution of A and B, which is correct?
A) ΔH_mix = 0 and ΔV_mix = 0
B) ΔH_mix > 0 and ΔV_mix > 0
C) ΔH_mix < 0 and ΔV_mix < 0
D) ΔH_mix = 0 but ΔV_mix ≠ 0
Answer: A [Explanation: Ideal solutions obey Raoult's law and have zero enthalpy and volume of mixing.]

Q: A solution of two liquids A and B shows negative deviation from Raoult's law. This means
A) A–B interactions are weaker than A–A and B–B
B) A–B interactions are stronger than A–A and B–B
C) A–B interactions are equal to A–A and B–B
D) ΔH_mix > 0
Answer: B [Explanation: Stronger intermolecular forces lead to negative deviation.]

Q: The vapour pressure of pure liquid A is 100 torr. On adding a non-volatile solute, vapour pressure becomes 90 torr. Mole fraction of solute is
A) 0.10
B) 0.90
C) 0.11
D) 0.05
Answer: A [Explanation: Relative lowering = x₂ = (100–90)/100 = 0.10]

Q: Which of the following aqueous solutions has the highest freezing point?
A) 0.1 M glucose
B) 0.1 M NaCl
C) 0.1 M BaCl₂
D) 0.1 M Al₂(SO₄)₃
Answer: A [Explanation: Lowest van't Hoff factor (i = 1) → least depression → highest freezing point.]

Q: 0.5 molal aqueous solution of a weak monobasic acid is 50% ionised. Freezing point of solution is (K_f = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹)
