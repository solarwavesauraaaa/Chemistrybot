Q: For the reaction 2A + B → C, the rate of disappearance of A is 2.0 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹. The rate of appearance of C is
A) 1.0 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
B) 2.0 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
C) 4.0 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
D) 0.5 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
Answer: A [Explanation: Rate = -½ d[A]/dt = d[C]/dt; hence rate of appearance of C is half the rate of disappearance of A.]

Q: The unit of rate constant for a second-order reaction is
A) mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
B) s⁻¹
C) L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
D) L² mol⁻² s⁻¹
Answer: C [Explanation: For order n, unit of k is (concentration)^(1-n) time⁻¹.]

Q: For a first-order reaction, the time required for 99% completion is how many times the time required for 90% completion?
A) 2
B) 3
C) 1.5
D) 4
Answer: A [Explanation: t₉₉% = (2.303/k) log100; t₉₀% = (2.303/k) log10; ratio = 2.]

Q: The half-life of a first-order reaction is 10 min. The time required for 87.5% completion is
A) 20 min
B) 30 min
C) 40 min
D) 50 min
Answer: B [Explanation: 87.5% completion requires 3 half-lives (50% → 75% → 87.5%).]

Q: For the reaction A → products, the concentration of A falls from 0.8 M to 0.4 M in 20 min and from 0.4 M to 0.2 M in another 20 min. The order of the reaction is
A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 3
Answer: B [Explanation: Equal time for concentration to become half → first order.]

Q: The rate constant of a reaction is 2.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ at 300 K and 8.0 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ at 320 K. The activation energy is approximately
A) 55 kJ mol⁻¹
B) 27.5 kJ mol⁻¹
C) 110 kJ mol⁻¹
D) 13.8 kJ mol⁻¹
Answer: A [Explanation: Use Arrhenius equation: log(k₂/k₁) = Eₐ/(2.303R) × (T₂-T₁)/(T₁T₂).]

Q: Assertion (A): Order of a reaction can be zero or fractional.
Reason (R): Molecularity of a reaction cannot be zero or fractional.
A) Both A and R true and R explains A
B) Both true but R does not explain A
C) A true, R false
D) A false, R true
Answer: B [Explanation: Both statements are independently correct; R does not explain A.]

Q: For a zero-order reaction, the plot of concentration vs time is
A) linear with positive slope
B) linear with negative slope
C) exponential curve
D) parabolic
Answer: B [Explanation: [A] = [A]₀ - kt; straight line with slope = -k.]

Q: The half-life of a zero-order reaction is directly proportional to
A) initial concentration
B) rate constant
C) inverse of initial concentration
D) square of initial concentration
Answer: A [Explanation: t₁/₂ = [A]₀/2k.]

Q: In a first-order reaction, if the concentration of reactant is increased 4 times, the rate becomes
A) 4 times
B) 2 times
C) 16 times
D) remains same
Answer: A [Explanation: Rate ∝ [A]; first-order dependence.]

Q: The integrated rate equation for a first-order reaction is
A) k = (2.303/t) log([A]₀/[A])
B) k = ([A]₀ - [A])/t
C) k = (1/t)(1/[A] - 1/[A]₀)
D) k = (2.303/t) log([A]/[A]₀)
Answer: A [Explanation: Standard integrated form for first order.]

Q: A reaction is 50% complete in 20 min and 75% complete in 40 min. The order of the reaction is
A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 3
Answer: B [Explanation: Constant half-life indicates first order.]

Q: The activation energy of a reaction is zero. The rate constant of the reaction
A) increases with increase in temperature
B) decreases with increase in temperature
C) is independent of temperature
D) first increases then decreases
Answer: C [Explanation: From Arrhenius equation, if Eₐ = 0, k is independent of T.]

Q: For the reaction 2N₂O₅ → 4NO₂ + O₂, the rate of formation of NO₂ is 1.6 × 10⁻³ mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹. The rate of disappearance of N₂O₅ is
A) 0.4 × 10⁻³
B) 0.8 × 10⁻³
C) 1.6 × 10⁻³
D) 3.2 × 10⁻³
Answer: B [Explanation: Rate = -½ d[N₂O₅]/dt = ¼ d[NO₂]/dt.]

Q: The slope of the plot of log k vs 1/T for a reaction is -5000. The activation energy is
A) 95.7 kJ mol⁻¹
B) 47.8 kJ mol⁻¹
C) 23.9 kJ mol⁻¹
D) 191.4 kJ mol⁻¹
Answer: A [Explanation: Slope = -Eₐ/2.303R; Eₐ = 5000 × 2.303 × 8.314 J ≈ 95.7 kJ.]

Q: Molecularity of a reaction
A) can be zero
B) can be fractional
C) is always a whole number
D) can be negative
Answer: C [Explanation: Molecularity is the number of molecules colliding and is always an integer.]

Q: For a second-order reaction, the half-life is
A) independent of initial concentration
B) directly proportional to initial concentration
C) inversely proportional to initial concentration
D) proportional to square of initial concentration
Answer: C [Explanation: t₁/₂ = 1/(k[A]₀).]

Q: The rate of a reaction becomes 4 times when temperature is raised from 300 K to 320 K. The energy of activation is
A) 50.1 kJ mol⁻¹
B) 25.05 kJ mol⁻¹
C) 100.2 kJ mol⁻¹
D) 12.5 kJ mol⁻¹
Answer: A [Explanation: Calculate using Arrhenius equation with k₂/k₁ = 4.]

Q: Assertion (A): The rate of a reaction increases with increase in temperature.
Reason (R): The number of effective collisions increases with increase in temperature.
A) Both A and R true and R explains A
B) Both true but R does not explain A
C) A true, R false
D) A false, R true
Answer: A [Explanation: Higher temperature increases both collision frequency and fraction of effective collisions.]

Q: In the reaction A + 2B → products, the rate law is rate = k[A][B]². The order of the reaction with respect to B is
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 0
Answer: B [Explanation: From the rate law, order w.r.t. B is 2.]

Q: The time for 90% completion of a first-order reaction is
A) t₁/₂
B) 2t₁/₂
C) 3.32t₁/₂
D) 10t₁/₂
Answer: C [Explanation: t₉₀% = (2.303/k)log10 = 2.303 × t₁/₂/0.693 ≈ 3.32t₁/₂.]

Q: A catalyst increases the rate of a reaction by
A) increasing activation energy
B) decreasing activation energy
C) increasing the number of collisions
D) changing the equilibrium constant
Answer: B [Explanation: Catalyst provides an alternative path of lower activation energy.]

Q: For a reaction, the plot of ln[A] vs time is a straight line with slope = -0.05 min⁻¹. The rate constant is
A) 0.05 min⁻¹
B) 0.115 min⁻¹
C) 0.023 min⁻¹
D) 0.002 min⁻¹
Answer: A [Explanation: For first order, slope of ln[A] vs t is -k.]

Q: The unit of rate of reaction is the same as that of rate constant for
A) zero-order reaction
B) first-order reaction
C) second-order reaction
D) third-order reaction
Answer: A [Explanation: For zero order, both have units of concentration time⁻¹.]

Q: In a first-order reaction, the concentration of reactant decreases from 0.8 M to 0.4 M in 15 min. The concentration after 45 min will be
A) 0.1 M

A) 70 kJ mol⁻¹
