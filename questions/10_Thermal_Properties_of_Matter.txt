# 10_Thermal_Properties_of_Matter

Q: The SI unit of heat is:
A) Joule
B) Calorie
C) Watt
D) Kelvin
Answer: A [Joule is the SI unit of heat and energy.]

Q: The temperature at which the volume of a gas becomes zero is:
A) 0 K
B) -273 K
C) 0 degrees C
D) 273 K
Answer: A [Absolute zero is 0 K, where volume of an ideal gas becomes zero.]

Q: The relationship between Celsius and Kelvin scales is:
A) K = C + 273
B) K = C - 273
C) K = C / 273
D) K = 273 - C
Answer: A [T(K) = T(C) + 273.]

Q: The coefficient of linear expansion (alpha) is defined as:
A) delta_L / (L * delta_T)
B) L / delta_T
C) delta_L * delta_T / L
D) delta_L / delta_T
Answer: A [alpha = (1/L) * (dL/dT).]

Q: The coefficient of volume expansion (gamma) is related to alpha for a solid by:
A) gamma = 3 alpha
B) gamma = alpha / 3
C) gamma = 2 alpha
D) gamma = alpha
Answer: A [For solids, gamma = 3 alpha.]

Q: The specific heat capacity of water is:
A) 4200 J/kg K
B) 1000 J/kg K
C) 2000 J/kg K
D) 8000 J/kg K
Answer: A [Specific heat of water is approximately 4200 J/kg K.]

Q: The amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of water by 1 K is:
A) 4200 J
B) 1000 J
C) 2000 J
D) 8000 J
Answer: A [1 kcal = 4200 J. So heat required = 4200 J.]

Q: The latent heat of fusion of ice is:
A) 80 cal/g
B) 540 cal/g
C) 100 cal/g
D) 200 cal/g
Answer: A [Latent heat of fusion of ice = 80 cal/g.]

Q: The latent heat of vaporization of water is:
A) 540 cal/g
B) 80 cal/g
C) 100 cal/g
D) 200 cal/g
Answer: A [Latent heat of vaporization = 540 cal/g.]

Q: A calorimeter works on the principle of:
A) Conservation of heat energy
B) Conservation of mass
C) Conservation of momentum
D) Conservation of volume
Answer: A [Calorimetry is based on the law of conservation of energy (heat).]

Q: The amount of heat required to change the temperature of a body is given by:
A) Q = m c delta_T
B) Q = m c / delta_T
C) Q = c delta_T / m
D) Q = delta_T / (m c)
Answer: A [Q = m c delta_T.]

Q: Which of the following is the best conductor of heat?
A) Silver
B) Copper
C) Aluminum
D) Iron
Answer: A [Silver is the best conductor of heat among common metals.]

Q: The thermal conductivity of a material is defined as the rate of heat flow per unit:
A) Temperature gradient per unit area
B) Area per unit temperature gradient
C) Length per unit area
D) Time per unit area
Answer: A [K = Q / (A * (dT/dx)).]

Q: The S.I. unit of thermal conductivity is:
A) W m^-1 K^-1
B) W m K^-1
C) W m^-1 K
D) W m K
Answer: A [Thermal conductivity unit: W m^-1 K^-1.]

Q: Conduction of heat takes place in:
A) Solids primarily
B) Liquids primarily
C) Gases primarily
D) Vacuum
Answer: A [Conduction is the main mode of heat transfer in solids.]

Q: Convection of heat takes place in:
A) Fluids (liquids and gases)
B) Solids only
C) Vacuum
D) Metals only
Answer: A [Convection occurs in fluids due to bulk movement.]

Q: Radiation of heat takes place through:
A) Electromagnetic waves
B) Conduction
C) Convection
D) Conduction and convection
Answer: A [Heat radiation is by electromagnetic waves (infrared).]

Q: The amount of heat radiated by a black body is proportional to:
A) T^4
B) T^3
C) T^2
D) T
Answer: A [Stefan-Boltzmann law: E ∝ T^4.]

Q: A black body is a perfect:
A) Absorber and emitter of radiation
B) Absorber only
C) Emitter only
D) Reflector
Answer: A [A black body absorbs all radiation and emits maximum radiation.]

Q: The emissive power of a black body is:
A) Maximum
B) Minimum
C) Zero
D) Independent of temperature
Answer: A [A black body has maximum emissive power at a given temperature.]

Q: The Wein's displacement law relates the wavelength of maximum emission to:
A) Temperature
B) Pressure
C) Volume
D) Density
Answer: A [lambda_max T = constant (Wein's law).]

Q: The wavelength of maximum emission for a black body at 300 K is (Wein's constant = 2.9e-3 m K):
A) 9.67e-6 m
B) 9.67e-5 m
C) 9.67e-4 m
D) 9.67e-3 m
Answer: A [lambda_max = b / T = 2.9e-3 / 300 = 9.67e-6 m = 9.67 µm.]

Q: The rate of heat loss from a body by radiation is given by:
A) Newton's law of cooling
B) Stefan's law
C) Wein's law
D) Kirchhoff's law
Answer: A [Newton's law of cooling: Rate of cooling ∝ temperature difference.]

Q: A good absorber of radiation is also a good:
A) Emitter
B) Conductor
C) Convector
D) Insulator
Answer: A [Kirchhoff's law: Good absorbers are good emitters.]

Q: The specific heat of a substance is 500 J/kg K. The heat required to raise the temperature of 2 kg of the substance by 10 K is:
A) 10000 J
B) 5000 J
C) 2000 J
D) 1000 J
Answer: A [Q = m c delta_T = 2 * 500 * 10 = 10000 J.]

Q: A body at 100 degrees C is placed in a room at 20 degrees C. The rate of cooling depends on:
A) Temperature difference
B) Surface area
C) Nature of surface
D) All of these
Answer: D [Newton's law of cooling: Rate depends on all these factors.]

Q: The thermal stress produced in a rod fixed between two rigid supports when heated by delta_T is:
A) Y alpha delta_T
B) Y alpha / delta_T
C) Y / (alpha delta_T)
D) Y alpha / delta_T^2
Answer: A [Thermal stress = Y alpha delta_T.]

Q: A metal rod of length L is heated by delta_T. If it is free to expand, the increase in length is:
A) L alpha delta_T
B) L alpha / delta_T
C) L / (alpha delta_T)
D) L delta_T / alpha
Answer: A [delta_L = L alpha delta_T.]

Q: A metal ring is heated. Its internal diameter:
A) Increases
B) Decreases
C) Remains the same
D) Becomes zero
Answer: A [The internal diameter increases on heating, just like expansion of the material.]

Q: The coefficient of area expansion (beta) is related to alpha by:
A) beta = 2 alpha
B) beta = 3 alpha
C) beta = alpha / 2
D) beta = alpha / 3
Answer: A [beta = 2 alpha for solids.]

Q: The value of gamma for an ideal gas is:
A) 1 / T
B) P / T
C) V / T
D) 1 / P
Answer: A [gamma = 1/T (for ideal gas volume expansion).]

Q: A thermometer measures temperature on the Celsius scale. At 0 degrees C, the corresponding Kelvin temperature is:
A) 273 K
B) 100 K
C) 0 K
D) 373 K
Answer: A [0 C = 273 K.]

Q: The triple point of water is:
A) 273.16 K
B) 273 K
C) 373 K
D) 0 K
Answer: A [Triple point of water = 273.16 K.]

Q: The heat capacity of a body depends on:
A) Its mass and specific heat
B) Its volume only
C) Its shape only
D) Its density only
Answer: A [Heat capacity = m * c.]

Q: Which of the following is a good thermal insulator?
A) Wool
B) Copper
C) Silver
D) Aluminum
Answer: A [Wool is a poor conductor, so it is a thermal insulator.]

Q: The mode of heat transfer from the Sun to Earth is:
A) Radiation
B) Conduction
C) Convection
D) Conduction and convection
Answer: A [Heat from Sun reaches Earth through radiation (vacuum).]

Q: The thermal conductivity of a good conductor is:
A) High
B) Low
C) Zero
D) Negative
Answer: A [Good conductors have high thermal conductivity.]

Q: The specific heat of a gas at constant pressure (C_p) is related to C_v by:
A) C_p - C_v = R
B) C_p + C_v = R
C) C_p * C_v = R
D) C_p / C_v = R
Answer: A [Mayer's relation: C_p - C_v = R.]

Q: The ratio of specific heats (gamma) for a monoatomic gas is:
A) 5/3
B) 7/5
C) 3/2
D) 4/3
Answer: A [gamma = C_p / C_v = 5/3 for monoatomic gas.]

Q: For a diatomic gas, the value of gamma is:
A) 7/5
B) 5/3
C) 3/2
D) 4/3
Answer: A [gamma = 7/5 for diatomic gas.]

Q: The heat required to raise the temperature of 1 mole of a gas by 1 K at constant volume is:
A) C_v
B) C_p
C) R
D) gamma
Answer: A [Molar specific heat at constant volume = C_v.]

Q: The heat required to raise the temperature of 1 mole of a gas by 1 K at constant pressure is:
A) C_p
B) C_v
C) R
D) gamma
Answer: A [Molar specific heat at constant pressure = C_p.]

Q: The adiabatic relation for an ideal gas is:
A) T V^(gamma-1) = constant
B) P V = constant
C) P V^gamma = constant
D) V T = constant
Answer: C [For adiabatic process: P V^gamma = constant.]

Q: During an isothermal process, the temperature of an ideal gas:
A) Remains constant
B) Increases
C) Decreases
D) Becomes zero
Answer: A [Isothermal means constant temperature.]
