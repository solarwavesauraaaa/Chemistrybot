# 14_Waves

Q: A wave in which the particles of the medium vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation is called:
A) Transverse wave
B) Longitudinal wave
C) Electromagnetic wave
D) Matter wave
Answer: A [In transverse waves, particles vibrate perpendicular to wave direction.]

Q: A wave in which the particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of wave propagation is called:
A) Longitudinal wave
B) Transverse wave
C) Electromagnetic wave
D) Matter wave
Answer: A [In longitudinal waves, particles vibrate parallel to wave direction.]

Q: Sound waves in air are:
A) Longitudinal waves
B) Transverse waves
C) Electromagnetic waves
D) Matter waves
Answer: A [Sound waves in air are longitudinal mechanical waves.]

Q: Light waves are:
A) Electromagnetic waves
B) Mechanical waves
C) Longitudinal waves
D) Matter waves
Answer: A [Light is an electromagnetic wave (transverse).]

Q: The speed of a wave is given by:
A) v = lambda * f
B) v = lambda / f
C) v = f / lambda
D) v = 1 / (lambda * f)
Answer: A [v = lambda * f.]

Q: The relation between wave velocity, angular frequency, and wave number is:
A) v = omega / k
B) v = omega * k
C) v = k / omega
D) v = omega^2 / k
Answer: A [v = omega / k.]

Q: The speed of a transverse wave on a stretched string is:
A) sqrt(T / mu)
B) sqrt(mu / T)
C) T / mu
D) mu / T
Answer: A [v = sqrt(T / mu).]

Q: The speed of a longitudinal wave in a solid is:
A) sqrt(Y / rho)
B) sqrt(rho / Y)
C) Y / rho
D) rho / Y
Answer: A [v = sqrt(Y / rho).]

Q: The speed of sound in air at 0°C is approximately:
A) 332 m/s
B) 340 m/s
C) 300 m/s
D) 350 m/s
Answer: A [v_sound at 0°C ≈ 332 m/s.]

Q: The speed of sound in air increases with:
A) Increase in temperature
B) Decrease in temperature
C) Increase in pressure
D) Decrease in pressure
Answer: A [v ∝ sqrt(T).]

Q: The phenomenon of bending of waves around obstacles is called:
A) Diffraction
B) Interference
C) Refraction
D) Reflection
Answer: A [Diffraction is bending of waves around obstacles.]

Q: The principle of superposition of waves states that:
A) The resultant displacement is the vector sum of individual displacements
B) Waves cancel each other
C) Waves add arithmetically
D) Waves do not interact
Answer: A [Superposition: resultant = sum of individual displacements.]

Q: When two waves of same frequency and amplitude overlap in phase, the resultant amplitude is:
A) 2A
B) A
C) 0
D) sqrt(2)A
Answer: A [Constructive interference: amplitude = A + A = 2A.]

Q: When two waves of same frequency and amplitude overlap in opposite phase, the resultant amplitude is:
A) 0
B) 2A
C) A
D) sqrt(2)A
Answer: A [Destructive interference: amplitude = A - A = 0.]

Q: The condition for constructive interference is:
A) Phase difference = 2npi
B) Phase difference = (2n+1)pi
C) Path difference = (2n+1)lambda/2
D) Both B and C
Answer: A [Constructive: delta_phi = 2npi.]

Q: The condition for destructive interference is:
A) Phase difference = (2n+1)pi
B) Phase difference = 2npi
C) Path difference = n lambda
D) Path difference = 2n lambda
Answer: A [Destructive: delta_phi = (2n+1)pi.]

Q: Standing waves are formed by the superposition of:
A) Two identical waves traveling in opposite directions
B) Two identical waves traveling in the same direction
C) Two different waves traveling in opposite directions
D) Two waves of different frequencies
Answer: A [Standing waves require two waves of same frequency moving in opposite directions.]

Q: In a standing wave, the points of zero amplitude are called:
A) Nodes
B) Antinodes
C) Crests
D) Troughs
Answer: A [Nodes are points with zero amplitude.]

Q: In a standing wave, the points of maximum amplitude are called:
A) Antinodes
B) Nodes
C) Crests
D) Troughs
Answer: A [Antinodes have maximum amplitude.]

Q: The distance between two consecutive nodes in a standing wave is:
A) lambda / 2
B) lambda / 4
C) lambda
D) 2 lambda
Answer: A [Distance between nodes = lambda/2.]

Q: The distance between two consecutive antinodes in a standing wave is:
A) lambda / 2
B) lambda / 4
C) lambda
D) 2 lambda
Answer: A [Distance between antinodes = lambda/2.]

Q: The distance between a node and an adjacent antinode is:
A) lambda / 4
B) lambda / 2
C) lambda
D) 2 lambda
Answer: A [Distance = lambda/4.]

Q: The fundamental frequency of a stretched string fixed at both ends is:
A) f_1 = v / 2L
B) f_1 = v / L
C) f_1 = 2v / L
D) f_1 = v / 4L
Answer: A [Fundamental frequency: f_1 = v / 2L.]

Q: The frequency of the nth harmonic of a stretched string is:
A) f_n = n v / 2L
B) f_n = v / 2L
C) f_n = n v / L
D) f_n = 2n v / L
Answer: A [f_n = n * f_1 = n v / 2L.]

Q: The fundamental frequency of a pipe open at both ends is:
A) f_1 = v / 2L
B) f_1 = v / 4L
C) f_1 = v / L
D) f_1 = 2v / L
Answer: A [For open pipe: f_1 = v / 2L.]

Q: The fundamental frequency of a pipe closed at one end is:
A) f_1 = v / 4L
B) f_1 = v / 2L
C) f_1 = v / L
D) f_1 = 2v / L
Answer: A [For closed pipe: f_1 = v / 4L.]

Q: The nth harmonic of an open pipe is:
A) f_n = n v / 2L
B) f_n = n v / 4L
C) f_n = (2n-1) v / 4L
D) f_n = 2n v / L
Answer: A [Open pipe: all harmonics present, f_n = n v / 2L.]

Q: The nth harmonic of a closed pipe is:
A) f_n = (2n-1) v / 4L
B) f_n = n v / 2L
C) f_n = n v / 4L
D) f_n = 2n v / L
Answer: A [Closed pipe: only odd harmonics, f_n = (2n-1) v / 4L.]

Q: The phenomenon of resonance occurs when:
A) The frequency of the driving force matches the natural frequency
B) The amplitude is zero
C) The frequency is very high
D) The frequency is very low
Answer: A [Resonance: driving frequency = natural frequency.]

Q: The Doppler effect is the change in:
A) Observed frequency due to relative motion
B) Speed of sound
C) Wavelength
D) Amplitude
Answer: A [Doppler effect: change in observed frequency due to motion.]

Q: The apparent frequency heard by a stationary observer when a source moves towards it is:
A) f' = f (v / (v - v_s))
B) f' = f (v / (v + v_s))
C) f' = f ((v + v_s) / v)
D) f' = f ((v - v_s) / v)
Answer: A [Source moving towards observer: f' = f * v / (v - v_s).]

Q: The apparent frequency heard by an observer moving towards a stationary source is:
A) f' = f ((v + v_o) / v)
B) f' = f (v / (v + v_o))
C) f' = f (v / (v - v_o))
D) f' = f ((v - v_o) / v)
Answer: A [Observer moving towards source: f' = f * (v + v_o) / v.]

Q: In the Doppler effect, if the source and observer move away from each other, the apparent frequency:
A) Decreases
B) Increases
C) Remains constant
D) Becomes zero
Answer: A [Moving away results in decreased observed frequency.]

Q: A source of sound moves towards a stationary observer with speed v_s. The apparent frequency is:
A) f' = f v / (v - v_s)
B) f' = f v / (v + v_s)
C) f' = f (v - v_s) / v
D) f' = f (v + v_s) / v
Answer: A [Standard formula for source approaching.]

Q: The speed of sound in a gas is given by:
A) v = sqrt(gamma P / rho)
B) v = sqrt(P / rho)
C) v = sqrt(gamma rho / P)
D) v = sqrt(RT / gamma)
Answer: A [v = sqrt(gamma P / rho) = sqrt(gamma RT/M).]

Q: The ratio of speeds of sound in two gases at the same temperature is:
A) sqrt(gamma1 M2 / (gamma2 M1))
B) sqrt(gamma1 / gamma2)
C) sqrt(M1 / M2)
D) gamma1 / gamma2
Answer: A [v = sqrt(gamma RT/M).]

Q: The intensity of a wave is proportional to:
A) Square of amplitude
B) Amplitude
C) Frequency
D) Wavelength
Answer: A [Intensity ∝ A^2.]

Q: The loudness of sound is related to:
A) Intensity
B) Frequency
C) Wavelength
D) Speed
Answer: A [Loudness ∝ log I.]

Q: The quality of a sound wave depends on:
A) Waveform (overtones)
B) Frequency
C) Intensity
D) Velocity
Answer: A [Quality/Timbre depends on the relative intensity of harmonics.]

Q: The pitch of a sound wave depends on:
A) Frequency
B) Intensity
C) Velocity
D) Wavelength
Answer: A [Pitch depends on frequency.]

Q: When a wave travels from one medium to another, which quantity remains unchanged?
A) Frequency
B) Wavelength
C) Speed
D) Amplitude
Answer: A [Frequency remains constant during refraction.]

Q: When a wave travels from one medium to another, the speed changes because of:
A) Change in medium properties
B) Change in frequency
C) Change in amplitude
D) Change in phase
Answer: A [Speed changes due to different elastic properties/density.]

Q: The wave equation for a wave traveling in the positive x-direction is:
A) y = A sin(kx - omega t + phi)
B) y = A sin(kx + omega t + phi)
C) y = A cos(kx + omega t)
D) y = A tan(kx - omega t)
Answer: A [Standard form: y = A sin(kx - omega t + phi).]

Q: The time period of a wave is 0.02 s. The frequency is:
A) 50 Hz
B) 20 Hz
C) 100 Hz
D) 200 Hz
Answer: A [f = 1/T = 1/0.02 = 50 Hz.]

Q: A wave has frequency 500 Hz and wavelength 0.68 m. The speed is:
A) 340 m/s
B) 500 m/s
C) 680 m/s
D) 250 m/s
Answer: A [v = f * lambda = 500 * 0.68 = 340 m/s.]

Q: In a standing wave, the energy is:
A) Stored between nodes and antinodes
B) Transmitted along the wave
C) Zero
D) Infinite
Answer: A [Energy is stored and not transmitted in standing waves.]
