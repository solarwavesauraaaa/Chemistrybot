# 17_Current_Electricity

Q: Electric current is defined as the rate of flow of:
A) Charge
B) Electrons
C) Protons
D) Neutrons
Answer: A [I = dQ/dt. Charge flowing per unit time.]

Q: The SI unit of electric current is:
A) Ampere
B) Coulomb
C) Volt
D) Ohm
Answer: A [Ampere (A) is the SI unit of current.]

Q: The direction of conventional current is:
A) Opposite to electron flow
B) Same as electron flow
C) Same as proton flow
D) Random
Answer: A [Conventional current flows from + to -, opposite to electron flow.]

Q: The resistance of a conductor depends on:
A) Length, area, and material
B) Only length
C) Only area
D) Only material
Answer: A [R = rho L / A.]

Q: The resistivity of a material depends on:
A) Temperature and material
B) Length
C) Area
D) Current
Answer: A [Resistivity is a material property, depends on temperature.]

Q: The SI unit of resistivity is:
A) Ohm meter
B) Ohm / meter
C) Ohm m^2
D) Ohm / m^2
Answer: A [rho = R A / L. Unit = Ohm m.]

Q: The conductivity of a material is:
A) sigma = 1 / rho
B) sigma = rho
C) sigma = R / L
D) sigma = L / R
Answer: A [Conductivity is reciprocal of resistivity.]

Q: Ohm's law states that:
A) V = I R
B) V = I / R
C) V = R / I
D) V = I + R
Answer: A [V = I R.]

Q: The resistance of a wire of length L and area A is R. If the length is doubled, the resistance becomes:
A) 2R
B) R/2
C) 4R
D) R/4
Answer: A [R ∝ L. So new resistance = 2R.]

Q: The resistance of a wire of length L and area A is R. If the area is doubled, the resistance becomes:
A) R/2
B) 2R
C) 4R
D) R/4
Answer: A [R ∝ 1/A. So new resistance = R/2.]

Q: The equivalent resistance of resistors in series is:
A) R_eq = R1 + R2 + ...
B) 1/R_eq = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ...
C) R_eq = R1 * R2 / (R1 + R2)
D) R_eq = R1 / R2
Answer: A [Series: R_eq = sum of resistances.]

Q: The equivalent resistance of resistors in parallel is:
A) 1/R_eq = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ...
B) R_eq = R1 + R2 + ...
C) R_eq = R1 * R2 / (R1 + R2)
D) R_eq = R1 / R2
Answer: A [Parallel: reciprocal of sum of reciprocals.]

Q: The internal resistance of a cell affects:
A) The terminal voltage
B) The emf
C) The current
D) All of these
Answer: A [Terminal voltage V = E - I r.]

Q: The emf of a cell is the potential difference when:
A) No current flows
B) Current is maximum
C) Current is zero
D) Current is infinite
Answer: A [EMF is P.D. when no current flows (open circuit).]

Q: The terminal voltage of a cell is:
A) V = E - I r
B) V = E + I r
C) V = E / I r
D) V = I r - E
Answer: A [V = E - I r.]

Q: The power dissipated in a resistor is:
A) P = I^2 R
B) P = V / R
C) P = I / R
D) P = V^2 * R
Answer: A [P = I^2 R = V^2 / R.]

Q: The SI unit of power is:
A) Watt
B) Joule
C) Volt
D) Ampere
Answer: A [Watt (W) is SI unit of electric power.]

Q: The heating effect of current is used in:
A) Electric heater
B) Electric fan
C) Electric motor
D) Electric generator
Answer: A [Heating effect is used in heaters, toasters, etc.]

Q: Joule's law of heating states that heat produced is proportional to:
A) I^2 R t
B) I R t
C) I^2 t / R
D) I / R t
Answer: A [H = I^2 R t.]

Q: The amount of heat produced in a resistor of 10 Ω carrying 2 A for 5 seconds is:
A) 200 J
B) 100 J
C) 50 J
D) 400 J
Answer: A [H = I^2 R t = 4 * 10 * 5 = 200 J.]

Q: The current in a circuit is 2 A when connected to a 10 V source. The resistance is:
A) 5 Ω
B) 10 Ω
C) 20 Ω
D) 2 Ω
Answer: A [R = V / I = 10 / 2 = 5 Ω.]

Q: The number of electrons flowing per second through a conductor carrying 1 A is:
A) 6.25 x 10^18
B) 1.6 x 10^19
C) 6.25 x 10^16
D) 1.6 x 10^18
Answer: A [1 A = 1 C/s. 1 C = 6.25 x 10^18 electrons.]

Q: The variation of resistance with temperature for a conductor is:
A) R = R0 (1 + alpha delta_T)
B) R = R0 (1 - alpha delta_T)
C) R = R0 / (1 + alpha delta_T)
D) R = R0 * alpha * delta_T
Answer: A [R = R0 (1 + alpha delta_T).]

Q: A material with negative temperature coefficient of resistance is:
A) Semiconductor
B) Metal
C) Superconductor
D) Insulator
Answer: A [Semiconductors have negative temp coefficient.]

Q: The resistivity of a conductor increases with:
A) Increase in temperature
B) Decrease in temperature
C) Increase in length
D) Decrease in area
Answer: A [For conductors, resistivity increases with temperature.]

Q: The resistivity of a semiconductor decreases with:
A) Increase in temperature
B) Decrease in temperature
C) Increase in length
D) Decrease in area
Answer: A [For semiconductors, resistivity decreases with temperature.]

Q: A cell of emf 12 V and internal resistance 2 Ω is connected to a 4 Ω resistor. The current in the circuit is:
A) 2 A
B) 3 A
C) 4 A
D) 1 A
Answer: A [I = E / (R + r) = 12 / (4 + 2) = 2 A.]

Q: Two resistors of 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in series. The equivalent resistance is:
A) 9 Ω
B) 2 Ω
C) 18 Ω
D) 4 Ω
Answer: A [R_eq = 6 + 3 = 9 Ω.]

Q: Two resistors of 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in parallel. The equivalent resistance is:
A) 2 Ω
B) 9 Ω
C) 18 Ω
D) 4 Ω
Answer: A [1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 -> R = 2 Ω.]

Q: A potentiometer is used to measure:
A) Potential difference accurately
B) Current accurately
C) Resistance accurately
D) Power accurately
Answer: A [Potentiometer measures potential difference without drawing current.]

Q: The principle of a potentiometer is:
A) V = I R
B) V = constant
C) Potential gradient is constant
D) Current is constant
Answer: C [Potential gradient = V/L is constant for a potentiometer wire.]

Q: A Wheatstone bridge is balanced when:
A) P / Q = R / S
B) P * Q = R * S
C) P + Q = R + S
D) P - Q = R - S
Answer: A [Balanced condition: P/Q = R/S.]

Q: The meter bridge works on the principle of:
A) Wheatstone bridge
B) Potentiometer
C) Ohm's law
D) Joule's law
Answer: A [Meter bridge is a practical form of Wheatstone bridge.]

Q: In a meter bridge, the null point is obtained at 40 cm from one end. The ratio of resistances is:
A) 2:3
B) 3:2
C) 4:6
D) 6:4
Answer: A [P/Q = 40/60 = 2/3.]

Q: The color code of a resistor is used to find:
A) Its resistance and tolerance
B) Its power rating
C) Its voltage rating
D) Its current rating
Answer: A [Color codes give resistance and tolerance.]

Q: The Kirchhoff's junction rule is based on:
A) Conservation of charge
B) Conservation of energy
C) Conservation of momentum
D) Conservation of mass
Answer: A [Junction rule (KCL): sum of currents entering = sum leaving.]

Q: The Kirchhoff's loop rule is based on:
A) Conservation of energy
B) Conservation of charge
C) Conservation of momentum
D) Conservation of mass
Answer: A [Loop rule (KVL): sum of potential changes = 0.]

Q: The power consumed by a 100 W bulb operated at 200 V is:
A) 100 W
B) 50 W
C) 200 W
D) 25 W
Answer: A [Power rating is for its rated voltage. At 200 V, it consumes 100 W.]

Q: The resistance of a 100 W bulb rated for 220 V is:
A) 484 Ω
B) 242 Ω
C) 121 Ω
D) 100 Ω
Answer: A [R = V^2 / P = 220^2 / 100 = 484 Ω.]

Q: If a 100 W bulb is operated at 110 V, the power consumed is:
A) 25 W
B) 50 W
C) 100 W
D) 200 W
Answer: A [P = V^2 / R = 110^2 / 484 = 25 W.]

Q: Two bulbs of 60 W and 100 W are connected in series. Which will glow brighter?
A) 60 W bulb
B) 100 W bulb
C) Both equally
D) Neither
Answer: A [In series, current is same. P = I^2 R. Higher R (lower wattage) dissipates more power.]

Q: Two bulbs of 60 W and 100 W are connected in parallel. Which will glow brighter?
A) 100 W bulb
B) 60 W bulb
C) Both equally
D) Neither
Answer: A [In parallel, voltage is same. P = V^2 / R. Lower R (higher wattage) dissipates more power.]

Q: The resistance of an ideal voltmeter is:
A) Infinity
B) Zero
C) 1 Ω
D) 100 Ω
Answer: A [Voltmeter has very high resistance (ideal = infinity).]

Q: The resistance of an ideal ammeter is:
A) Zero
B) Infinity
C) 1 Ω
D) 100 Ω
Answer: A [Ammeter has very low resistance (ideal = zero).]

Q: The current through a conductor is 5 A for 2 minutes. The charge flowing is:
A) 600 C
B) 1200 C
C) 300 C
D) 150 C
Answer: A [Q = I * t = 5 * 120 = 600 C.]

Q: A 4 Ω resistor is connected to a 12 V battery. The current is:
A) 3 A
B) 2 A
C) 4 A
D) 1 A
Answer: A [I = V / R = 12 / 4 = 3 A.]

Q: Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω, and 6 Ω are connected in parallel. The equivalent resistance is:
A) 1 Ω
B) 2 Ω
C) 3 Ω
D) 6 Ω
Answer: A [1/R = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 1 -> R = 1 Ω.]

Q: The conductivity of a material is measured in:
A) S/m
B) Ohm m
C) S m
D) Ohm / m
Answer: A [Conductivity unit: Siemens per meter (S/m).]

Q: A wire of resistance 4 Ω is stretched to twice its length. The new resistance is:
A) 16 Ω
B) 8 Ω
C) 4 Ω
D) 2 Ω
Answer: A [Volume constant: L doubles, A halves. R = 4 times = 16 Ω.]

Q: The current through a 10 Ω resistor is 0.5 A. The voltage across it is:
A) 5 V
B) 10 V
C) 20 V
D) 2 V
Answer: A [V = I R = 0.5 * 10 = 5 V.]

Q: The power dissipated in a 10 Ω resistor carrying 2 A is:
A) 40 W
B) 20 W
C) 10 W
D) 80 W
Answer: A [P = I^2 R = 4 * 10 = 40 W.]

Q: A 12 V battery is connected to a 6 Ω resistor. The power consumed is:
A) 24 W
B) 12 W
C) 36 W
D) 48 W
Answer: A [P = V^2 / R = 144 / 6 = 24 W.]

Q: The number of joules in 1 kWh is:
A) 3.6 x 10^6 J
B) 3.6 x 10^3 J
C) 3.6 x 10^5 J
D) 3.6 x 10^7 J
Answer: A [1 kWh = 1000 W * 3600 s = 3.6 x 10^6 J.]
