# 24_Wave_Optics

Q: The wave nature of light was first proposed by:
A) Huygens
B) Newton
C) Einstein
D) Planck
Answer: A [Huygens proposed the wave theory of light.]

Q: The phenomenon of interference of light is due to:
A) Wave nature of light
B) Particle nature of light
C) Dual nature of light
D) Quantum nature of light
Answer: A [Interference is a wave phenomenon.]

Q: The principle of superposition of waves states that:
A) The resultant displacement is the sum of individual displacements
B) Waves cancel each other
C) Waves add arithmetically
D) Waves do not interact
Answer: A [Superposition: resultant = sum of individual displacements.]

Q: In constructive interference, the path difference is:
A) n lambda
B) (2n+1) lambda / 2
C) n lambda / 2
D) (2n+1) lambda
Answer: A [Constructive: path difference = n lambda.]

Q: In destructive interference, the path difference is:
A) (2n+1) lambda / 2
B) n lambda
C) n lambda / 2
D) (2n+1) lambda
Answer: A [Destructive: path difference = (2n+1) lambda/2.]

Q: The condition for constructive interference in terms of phase difference is:
A) Delta_phi = 2n pi
B) Delta_phi = (2n+1) pi
C) Delta_phi = n pi
D) Delta_phi = (2n+1) pi/2
Answer: A [Constructive: phase diff = 2n pi.]

Q: The condition for destructive interference in terms of phase difference is:
A) Delta_phi = (2n+1) pi
B) Delta_phi = 2n pi
C) Delta_phi = n pi
D) Delta_phi = (2n+1) pi/2
Answer: A [Destructive: phase diff = (2n+1) pi.]

Q: In Young's double slit experiment, the fringe width is:
A) beta = lambda D / d
B) beta = lambda d / D
C) beta = d D / lambda
D) beta = D / (lambda d)
Answer: A [Fringe width beta = lambda D / d.]

Q: In Young's double slit experiment, if the slit separation is doubled, the fringe width becomes:
A) Half
B) Double
C) Four times
D) One-fourth
Answer: A [beta ∝ 1/d. If d doubles, beta halves.]

Q: In Young's double slit experiment, if the distance between slits and screen is halved, the fringe width becomes:
A) Half
B) Double
C) Four times
D) One-fourth
Answer: A [beta ∝ D. If D halves, beta halves.]

Q: In Young's double slit experiment, if the wavelength of light is doubled, the fringe width becomes:
A) Double
B) Half
C) Four times
D) One-fourth
Answer: A [beta ∝ lambda. If lambda doubles, beta doubles.]

Q: In Young's double slit experiment, the central fringe is:
A) Bright
B) Dark
C) Zero intensity
D) Half intensity
Answer: A [Central fringe is bright (constructive interference).]

Q: In Young's double slit experiment, the intensity of the central maximum is I0. If one slit is closed, the intensity becomes:
A) I0 / 4
B) I0 / 2
C) I0
D) 2 I0
Answer: A [With two slits, I = 4I_single. So single slit intensity = I0/4.]

Q: The condition for maxima in single slit diffraction is:
A) a sin(theta) = n lambda
B) a sin(theta) = (2n+1) lambda / 2
C) a sin(theta) = n lambda / 2
D) a sin(theta) = (2n+1) lambda
Answer: A [Diffraction maxima: a sin(theta) = n lambda.]

Q: The condition for minima in single slit diffraction is:
A) a sin(theta) = n lambda
B) a sin(theta) = (2n+1) lambda / 2
C) a sin(theta) = n lambda / 2
D) a sin(theta) = (2n+1) lambda
Answer: A [Diffraction minima: a sin(theta) = n lambda.]

Q: The width of the central maximum in single slit diffraction is:
A) 2 lambda D / a
B) lambda D / a
C) D / a
D) a / D
Answer: A [Width of central maximum = 2 lambda D / a.]

Q: The phenomenon of diffraction is observed when:
A) The slit width is comparable to the wavelength
B) The slit width is much larger than the wavelength
C) The slit width is much smaller than the wavelength
D) The slit width is zero
Answer: A [Diffraction is prominent when slit size ~ wavelength.]

Q: The resolving power of a microscope is given by:
A) RP = 2 n sin(theta) / lambda
B) RP = n sin(theta) / lambda
C) RP = lambda / (2 n sin(theta))
D) RP = 2 lambda / (n sin(theta))
Answer: A [RP ∝ 1/lambda and ∝ NA.]

Q: The resolving power of a telescope is:
A) RP = D / 1.22 lambda
B) RP = 1.22 lambda / D
C) RP = D / lambda
D) RP = lambda / D
Answer: A [RP = D / 1.22 lambda.]

Q: The polarization of light is a property of:
A) Transverse waves
B) Longitudinal waves
C) Both transverse and longitudinal
D) Neither
Answer: A [Polarization occurs only for transverse waves.]

Q: Unpolarized light can be polarized by:
A) A polaroid
B) A prism
C) A lens
D) A mirror
Answer: A [Polaroid selectively transmits light with a specific polarization.]

Q: When unpolarized light passes through a polaroid, the transmitted light is:
A) Polarized
B) Unpolarized
C) Partially polarized
D) Completely unpolarized
Answer: A [Transmitted light from a polaroid is plane polarized.]

Q: According to Malus's law, the intensity of polarized light transmitted through an analyzer is:
A) I = I0 cos^2(theta)
B) I = I0 sin^2(theta)
C) I = I0 cos(theta)
D) I = I0 sin(theta)
Answer: A [Malus's law: I = I0 cos^2(theta).]

Q: When the angle between the polarizer and analyzer is 90°, the transmitted intensity is:
A) Zero
B) Maximum
C) Half
D) One-fourth
Answer: A [cos(90°) = 0, so I = 0 (crossed polarizers).]

Q: When the angle between the polarizer and analyzer is 0°, the transmitted intensity is:
A) Maximum
B) Zero
C) Half
D) One-fourth
Answer: A [cos(0°) = 1, so I = I0 (parallel polarizers).]

Q: Brewster's law relates:
A) The polarizing angle to the refractive index
B) The critical angle to the refractive index
C) The angle of deviation to the prism angle
D) The angle of diffraction to the wavelength
Answer: A [tan(theta_p) = n.]

Q: At the polarizing angle, the reflected and refracted rays are:
A) Perpendicular to each other
B) Parallel to each other
C) Anti-parallel
D) At 45° to each other
Answer: A [At Brewster's angle, reflected and refracted rays are 90° apart.]

Q: The wavefront is a surface of:
A) Constant phase
B) Constant amplitude

Q: The shape of a wavefront from a point source is:

Q: The shape of a wavefront from a distant source is:
A) Plane

C) Cylindrical
D) Elliptical

Q: Huygens' principle states that:
A) Each point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets
