Q: An ideal gas expands isothermally from 2 L to 10 L against vacuum. Which statement is rigorously correct?
A) q = +w and ΔU > 0
B) q = -w = 0 and ΔU = 0
C) w = -p_exΔV with p_ex > 0
D) The process is reversible
Answer: B
[Explanation:] Free expansion (p_ex=0) gives w=0. Joule's experiment shows q=0 for isothermal free expansion of an ideal gas; hence ΔU=0.

Q: For an isothermal reversible expansion of 1 mol ideal gas from V_i to V_f at temperature T, the work done by the system is
A) nRT ln(V_f/V_i)
B) -nRT ln(V_f/V_i)
C) p_ex(V_f - V_i)
D) zero
Answer: A
[Explanation:] w_rev = -nRT ln(V_f/V_i) (work done on the system). Work done by the system is therefore +nRT ln(V_f/V_i).

Q: In a closed system, 500 J of heat is absorbed and 200 J of work is done by the system. ΔU is
A) +700 J
B) +300 J
C) -300 J
D) -700 J
Answer: B
[Explanation:] First law (IUPAC): ΔU = q + w. q = +500 J, w = -200 J ⇒ ΔU = +300 J.

Q: Which quantity is always a state function?
A) q
B) w
C) q + w
D) path-dependent heat at constant pressure
Answer: C
[Explanation:] ΔU = q + w depends only on initial and final states; q and w individually are path-dependent.

Q: For the isothermal irreversible compression of an ideal gas by a constant external pressure, the magnitude of work done on the system is
A) greater than that for reversible compression between the same volumes
B) smaller than that for reversible compression between the same volumes
C) equal to the reversible work
D) independent of external pressure
Answer: A
[Explanation:] On a p-V plot the area under the irreversible path (constant p_ex) is larger than the area under the reversible isotherm; hence more work is done on the system.

Q: ΔH = ΔU + Δn_gRT is valid only when
A) the reaction occurs at constant volume
B) all gases behave ideally and temperature is constant
C) pressure is not constant
D) solids and liquids are also present in large amounts
Answer: B
[Explanation:] Derivation assumes ideal-gas law for the gaseous species and constant T so that pΔV = Δn_gRT.

Q: For the reaction H₂O(l) → H₂O(g) at 373 K and 1 bar, Δ_vapH = 40.79 kJ mol⁻¹. ΔU is approximately
A) 40.79 kJ mol⁻¹
B) 37.7 kJ mol⁻¹
C) 43.9 kJ mol⁻¹
D) zero
Answer: B
[Explanation:] Δn_g = +1, ΔU = ΔH - Δn_gRT = 40.79 - 1 × 8.314 × 10⁻³ × 373 ≈ 37.7 kJ mol⁻¹.

Q: Which of the following is an intensive property?
A) Internal energy
B) Enthalpy
C) Heat capacity
D) Molar heat capacity
Answer: D
[Explanation:] Molar quantities are intensive; U, H and total heat capacity are extensive.

Q: For an ideal gas, C_p - C_V equals
A) R only for monatomic gases
B) R for all ideal gases
C) zero
D) γR
Answer: B
[Explanation:] From ΔH = ΔU + Δ(nRT) one obtains C_p = C_V + R for any ideal gas.

Q: In a bomb calorimeter the measured heat change equals
A) ΔH
B) ΔU
C) q_p
D) zero because volume is constant
Answer: B
[Explanation:] Bomb calorimeter operates at constant volume; q_V = ΔU.

Q: 1.0 g graphite is burnt in a bomb calorimeter (C = 20.7 kJ K⁻¹). Temperature rises by 1 K. ΔU for combustion of 1 mol graphite is
A) -20.7 kJ mol⁻¹
B) -248.4 kJ mol⁻¹
C) +20.7 kJ mol⁻¹
D) -12.0 kJ mol⁻¹
Answer: B
[Explanation:] Heat released for 1 g = -20.7 kJ; for 12 g = -248.4 kJ mol⁻¹. Since Δn_g = 0, ΔH = ΔU.

Q: Standard state of a pure substance is defined as
A) 1 atm and 298 K only
B) pure form at 1 bar and a specified temperature
C) 1 M solution at 1 bar
D) any pressure and 0 K
Answer: B
[Explanation:] NCERT defines standard state as the pure substance at 1 bar and the temperature of interest (commonly 298 K).

Q: Enthalpy of fusion is always
A) negative
B) positive
C) zero at the melting point
D) equal to enthalpy of vaporisation
Answer: B
[Explanation:] Melting is endothermic; Δ_fusH° > 0.

Q: For an adiabatic process in an isolated system
A) q = 0 and ΔU = w
B) w = 0 and ΔU = q
C) q = w = 0
D) ΔU = 0 only if the gas is ideal
Answer: A

Q: Which process is reversible?
A) Free expansion of an ideal gas
B) Compression by a finite constant external pressure
C) Infinitely slow expansion against a continuously adjusted external pressure equal to the gas pressure
D) Sudden mixing of two gases
Answer: C
[Explanation:] Only a process that can be reversed by an infinitesimal change in an external variable is thermodynamically reversible.

Q: When 2 L of an ideal gas at 10 atm expands isothermally against a constant external pressure of 1 atm to a final volume of 10 L, q equals
A) zero
B) 8 L atm
C) -8 L atm
D) 39.4 L atm
Answer: B
[Explanation:] w = -p_exΔV = -1 × 8 = -8 L atm; for isothermal ideal-gas expansion ΔU = 0, so q = -w = +8 L atm.

Q: The relationship ΔH = ΔU + pΔV is valid for
A) any process at constant pressure
B) only ideal-gas reactions
C) only isothermal processes
D) only adiabatic processes
Answer: A
[Explanation:] Derived directly from the definition H = U + pV at constant pressure.

Q: Which pair consists of both extensive properties?
A) Temperature and pressure
B) Density and molar volume
C) Internal energy and enthalpy
D) Specific heat and refractive index
Answer: C
[Explanation:] U and H scale with the amount of substance; the others are intensive.

Q: For the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g) at constant T and p, ΔH - ΔU equals
A) +2RT
B) -2RT

D) zero
Answer: B
[Explanation:] Δn_g = 2 - 4 = -2; ΔH = ΔU + Δn_gRT ⇒ ΔH - ΔU = -2RT.

Q: In the first-law expression ΔU = q + w, the IUPAC sign convention assigns
A) positive w when work is done by the system
B) positive w when work is done on the system
C) positive q when heat leaves the system
D) both q and w always positive
Answer: B
[Explanation:] IUPAC convention: w > 0 for work done on the system; q > 0 for heat absorbed by the system.

Q: An adiabatic wall
A) allows heat transfer but not matter
B) allows neither heat nor matter transfer
C) allows matter transfer but not heat
D) is the same as a diathermic wall
Answer: B
[Explanation:] Adiabatic wall prevents heat transfer; an isolated system is closed by adiabatic walls.

Q: For an ideal gas undergoing isothermal expansion, ΔU = 0 because
A) q = 0
B) the internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature
C) w = 0
D) the process is reversible
Answer: B
[Explanation:] U of an ideal gas is a function of T alone; constant T forces ΔU = 0.

Q: The heat capacity measured in a constant-pressure calorimeter is
A) C_V
B) C_p

D) always equal to R
Answer: B
[Explanation:] At constant pressure q_p = C_pΔT = ΔH.
