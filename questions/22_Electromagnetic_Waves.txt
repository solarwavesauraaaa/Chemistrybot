# 22_Electromagnetic_Waves

Q: Electromagnetic waves are produced by:
A) Accelerating charges
B) Stationary charges
C) Uniformly moving charges
D) Charges at rest
Answer: A [Accelerating charges produce electromagnetic waves.]

Q: Electromagnetic waves consist of:
A) Oscillating electric and magnetic fields
B) Only electric fields
C) Only magnetic fields
D) Mechanical waves
Answer: A [EM waves are coupled oscillating E and B fields.]

Q: Electromagnetic waves are:
A) Transverse waves
B) Longitudinal waves
C) Surface waves
D) Matter waves
Answer: A [E and B fields are perpendicular to direction of propagation, so transverse.]

Q: The speed of electromagnetic waves in vacuum is:
A) c = 3 × 10^8 m/s
B) c = 3 × 10^10 m/s
C) c = 3 × 10^6 m/s
D) c = 3 × 10^4 m/s
Answer: A [Speed of light in vacuum c ≈ 3 × 10^8 m/s.]

Q: The relation between c, mu0, and epsilon0 is:
A) c = 1 / sqrt(mu0 epsilon0)
B) c = sqrt(mu0 epsilon0)
C) c = mu0 epsilon0
D) c = 1 / (mu0 epsilon0)
Answer: A [c = 1 / sqrt(mu0 epsilon0).]

Q: In an electromagnetic wave, the ratio of E to B is:
A) E / B = c
B) E / B = 1/c
C) E / B = c^2
D) E / B = 1/c^2
Answer: A [E = c B, so E/B = c.]

Q: The energy in an electromagnetic wave is:
A) Equally divided between E and B fields
B) Mostly in E field
C) Mostly in B field
D) Zero
Answer: A [Energy density = 0.5 epsilon0 E^2 + 0.5 B^2 / mu0, and both contribute equally.]

Q: The average energy density of an electromagnetic wave is:
A) u_avg = 0.5 epsilon0 E0^2
B) u_avg = epsilon0 E0^2
C) u_avg = 0.25 epsilon0 E0^2
D) u_avg = 2 epsilon0 E0^2
Answer: A [Average energy density = 0.5 epsilon0 E0^2.]

Q: The intensity of an electromagnetic wave is proportional to:
A) E0^2
B) E0
C) E0^3
D) 1 / E0^2
Answer: A [Intensity ∝ E0^2.]

Q: The Poynting vector represents:
A) The direction and magnitude of energy flow
B) The electric field vector
C) The magnetic field vector
D) The wave vector
Answer: A [S = (1/mu0) E × B gives energy flow direction and magnitude.]

Q: The SI unit of the Poynting vector is:
A) W/m^2
B) W/m
C) W m^2
D) W
Answer: A [W/m^2 (power per unit area).]

Q: The electromagnetic wave spectrum is arranged in order of:
A) Increasing frequency
B) Decreasing frequency
C) Increasing wavelength
D) Both A and C depending on direction
Answer: A [From radio waves (low f) to gamma rays (high f).]

Q: Which of the following has the highest frequency?
A) Gamma rays
B) X-rays
C) Ultraviolet
D) Visible light
Answer: A [Gamma rays have the highest frequency.]

Q: Which of the following has the longest wavelength?
A) Radio waves
B) Microwaves
C) Infrared
D) Ultraviolet
Answer: A [Radio waves have the longest wavelength.]

Q: Radio waves are produced by:
A) Oscillating circuits
B) Hot bodies
C) Nuclear transitions
D) X-ray tubes
Answer: A [Radio waves are generated by oscillating LC circuits.]

Q: Microwaves are used in:
A) Radar and communication
B) Heating and cooking
C) Medical imaging
D) All of these
Answer: D [Microwaves are used in radar, microwave ovens, and communication.]

Q: Infrared waves are produced by:
A) Hot bodies and molecules
B) Radioactive decay
C) Nuclear reactions
D) Electron transitions
Answer: A [Infrared radiation is emitted by hot objects and molecular vibrations.]

Q: Infrared waves are used in:
A) Thermal imaging and remote controls
B) Communication
C) Medical imaging
D) Cooking
Answer: A [Infrared is used in thermal imaging and remote controls.]

Q: Visible light is detected by the:
A) Human eye
B) Radio receivers
C) Infrared sensors
D) X-ray detectors
Answer: A [Visible light is perceived by the human eye.]

Q: The wavelength of visible light ranges from:
A) 400 nm to 700 nm
B) 100 nm to 400 nm
C) 700 nm to 1 mm
D) 1 mm to 1 m
Answer: A [Visible spectrum: 400 nm (violet) to 700 nm (red).]

Q: Ultraviolet (UV) rays are produced by:
A) Very hot bodies and discharge tubes
B) Radioactive decay
C) Nuclear reactions
D) Oscillating circuits
Answer: A [UV rays are emitted by very hot bodies and electric arcs.]

Q: UV rays are used for:
A) Sterilization and vitamin D production
B) Communication
C) Cooking
D) Medical imaging
Answer: A [UV is used for sterilization and helps produce vitamin D.]

Q: X-rays are produced by:
A) X-ray tubes (accelerated electrons hitting a target)
B) Hot bodies
C) Nuclear reactions
D) Oscillating circuits
Answer: A [X-rays are produced when high-speed electrons strike a metal target.]

Q: X-rays are used in:
A) Medical imaging and crystallography
B) Communication
C) Cooking
D) Remote control
Answer: A [X-rays are used for medical imaging and studying crystal structures.]

Q: Gamma rays are produced by:
A) Nuclear reactions and radioactive decay
B) Hot bodies
C) X-ray tubes
D) Oscillating circuits
Answer: A [Gamma rays are emitted during nuclear transitions.]

Q: Gamma rays are used in:
A) Cancer therapy and sterilization
B) Communication
C) Cooking
D) Medical imaging
Answer: A [Gamma rays are used in cancer treatment and sterilization.]

Q: Electromagnetic waves can travel through:
A) Vacuum
B) Air
C) Water
D) All of these
Answer: D [EM waves travel through vacuum and transparent media.]

Q: The refractive index of a medium is related to the speed of light by:
A) n = c / v
B) n = v / c
C) n = c * v
D) n = v * c
Answer: A [n = c / v.]

Q: The wavelength of EM waves in a medium is:
A) lambda_m = lambda / n
B) lambda_m = lambda * n
C) lambda_m = lambda / n^2
D) lambda_m = lambda * n^2
Answer: A [lambda_m = lambda / n.]

Q: The frequency of EM waves in a medium is:
A) Same as in vacuum
B) Different from vacuum
C) Zero
D) Infinite
Answer: A [Frequency remains constant when EM wave enters a medium.]

Q: The energy of a photon is given by:
A) E = h f
B) E = h / f
C) E = f / h
D) E = h c / f
Answer: A [E = h f.]

Q: The momentum of a photon is:
A) p = h / lambda
B) p = h lambda
C) p = h / c
D) p = c / h
Answer: A [p = h / lambda.]

Q: The electromagnetic spectrum includes (in order of increasing wavelength):
A) Gamma, X-ray, UV, Visible, IR, Microwave, Radio
B) Radio, Microwave, IR, Visible, UV, X-ray, Gamma
C) UV, X-ray, Gamma, Visible, IR, Radio
D) X-ray, Gamma, UV, Visible, IR, Microwave, Radio
Answer: A [Correct order: Gamma, X, UV, Visible, IR, Microwave, Radio.]

Q: The ozone layer protects us from:
A) UV rays
B) X-rays
C) Gamma rays
D) Infrared rays
Answer: A [Ozone absorbs UV radiation, protecting life on Earth.]

Q: The greenhouse effect is caused by:
A) Infrared radiation trapping
B) UV radiation trapping
C) Visible light trapping
D) X-rays trapping
Answer: A [Greenhouse gases trap infrared radiation, warming the Earth.]

Q: A sinusoidal EM wave has E field amplitude 100 V/m. The B field amplitude is:
A) 3.33 × 10^-7 T
B) 3 × 10^8 T
C) 3.33 × 10^7 T
D) 3 × 10^-8 T
Answer: A [B0 = E0 / c = 100 / (3e8) = 3.33e-7 T.]

Q: A radio station broadcasts at 100 MHz. The wavelength is:
A) 3 m
B) 0.3 m
C) 30 m
D) 300 m
Answer: A [lambda = c / f = 3e8 / 1e8 = 3 m.]

Q: A radar operates at 10 GHz. The wavelength is:
A) 3 cm
B) 0.3 cm
C) 30 cm
D) 300 cm
Answer: A [lambda = 3e8 / 1e10 = 0.03 m = 3 cm.]

Q: A photon has energy 3.3 eV. Its frequency is (h = 4.14e-15 eV s):
A) 7.97 × 10^14 Hz
B) 8 × 10^14 Hz
C) 3 × 10^14 Hz
D) 5 × 10^14 Hz
Answer: A [f = E / h = 3.3 / 4.14e-15 = 7.97e14 Hz.]

Q: The range of X-ray wavelengths is:
A) 10^-11 m to 10^-8 m
B) 10^-15 m to 10^-12 m
C) 10^-9 m to 10^-7 m
D) 10^-6 m to 10^-3 m
Answer: A [X-rays range from 0.01 nm to 10 nm (10^-11 to 10^-8 m).]

Q: The range of ultraviolet wavelengths is:
A) 10 nm to 400 nm
B) 400 nm to 700 nm
C) 700 nm to 1 mm
D) 10^-12 m to 10^-10 m
Answer: A [UV: 10 nm to 400 nm.]

Q: The range of radio wave wavelengths is:
A) 1 m to 10^5 m
B) 1 cm to 10 m
C) 10^-3 m to 1 m
D) 10^-6 m to 10^-3 m
Answer: A [Radio waves can have wavelengths from 1 m to over 10^5 m.]

Q: In an EM wave, the electric field and magnetic field are:
A) In phase
B) 90° out of phase
C) 180° out of phase
D) 45° out of phase
Answer: A [E and B are in phase in an EM wave.]

Q: The speed of EM waves in a medium of refractive index n is:
A) v = c / n
B) v = n c
C) v = c n
D) v = c / n^2
Answer: A [v = c / n.]

Q: The relation between energy and wavelength of a photon is:
A) E = h c / lambda
B) E = h c lambda
C) E = h / c lambda
D) E = h lambda / c
Answer: A [E = h c / lambda.]

Q: The momentum of a photon of wavelength 500 nm is:
A) 1.325 × 10^-27 kg m/s
B) 1.325 × 10^-24 kg m/s
C) 1.325 × 10^-30 kg m/s
D) 1.325 × 10^-21 kg m/s
Answer: A [p = h / lambda = 6.626e-34 / 5e-7 = 1.325e-27 kg m/s.]

Q: The electric field and magnetic field vectors in an EM wave are:
A) Perpendicular to each other
B) Parallel to each other
C) Anti-parallel
D) At 45° to each other
Answer: A [E and B are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.]

Q: The direction of propagation of an EM wave is along:
A) The direction of E × B
B) The direction of E
C) The direction of B
D) The direction of E + B
Answer: A [Propagation direction is along E × B (Poynting vector).]
