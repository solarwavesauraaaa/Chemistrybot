# 27_Nuclei

Q: The nucleus of an atom consists of:
A) Protons and neutrons
B) Protons and electrons
C) Electrons and neutrons
D) Only protons
Answer: A [The nucleus contains protons and neutrons (collectively called nucleons).]

Q: The mass number (A) of a nucleus is:
A) The total number of protons and neutrons
B) The number of protons
C) The number of neutrons
D) The number of electrons
Answer: A [A = Z + N.]

Q: The atomic number (Z) of a nucleus is:
A) The number of protons
B) The number of neutrons
C) The number of nucleons
D) The number of electrons
Answer: A [Z is the number of protons.]

Q: The neutron number (N) is:
A) A - Z
B) Z - A
C) A + Z
D) Z / A
Answer: A [N = A - Z.]

Q: The isotopes of an element have:
A) Same atomic number, different mass number
B) Same mass number, different atomic number
C) Same atomic number, same mass number
D) Different atomic number, different mass number
Answer: A [Isotopes have same Z, different A.]

Q: The isobars have:
A) Same mass number, different atomic number
B) Same atomic number, different mass number
C) Same atomic number, same mass number
D) Different atomic number, different mass number
Answer: A [Isobars have same A, different Z.]

Q: The isotones have:
A) Same neutron number, different atomic number
B) Same atomic number, different mass number
C) Same mass number, different atomic number
D) Same atomic number, same mass number
Answer: A [Isotones have same N, different Z.]

Q: The radius of a nucleus is given by:
A) R = R0 A^(1/3)
B) R = R0 A^(2/3)
C) R = R0 A
D) R = R0 / A
Answer: A [R = R0 A^(1/3), where R0 = 1.2 × 10^-15 m.]

Q: The density of a nucleus is:
A) Approximately constant for all nuclei
B) Varies with A
C) Varies with Z
D) Varies with N
Answer: A [Nuclear density is roughly constant (~2.3 × 10^17 kg/m^3).]

Q: The nuclear force is:
A) Attractive and short-range
B) Repulsive and long-range
C) Attractive and long-range
D) Repulsive and short-range
Answer: A [Nuclear force is attractive at short range (~10^-15 m).]

Q: The mass defect of a nucleus is:
A) The difference between the mass of the nucleus and the sum of masses of its nucleons
B) The sum of masses of its nucleons
C) The mass of the nucleus
D) The number of nucleons
Answer: A [Mass defect delta_m = [Z m_p + (A-Z) m_n] - M_nucleus.]

Q: The binding energy of a nucleus is:
A) E_b = delta_m c^2
B) E_b = delta_m / c^2
C) E_b = delta_m c
D) E_b = delta_m / c
Answer: A [E_b = delta_m c^2.]

Q: The binding energy per nucleon is:
A) E_b / A
B) E_b * A
C) E_b / Z
D) E_b / N
Answer: A [Binding energy per nucleon = E_b / A.]

Q: The binding energy per nucleon is maximum for:
A) Iron (Fe)
B) Hydrogen (H)
C) Uranium (U)
D) Helium (He)
Answer: A [Iron (A ≈ 56) has the highest binding energy per nucleon (~8.8 MeV).]

Q: Nuclear fission is the process in which:
A) A heavy nucleus splits into lighter nuclei
B) Light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus
C) A nucleus emits alpha particles
D) A nucleus emits beta particles
Answer: A [Fission: heavy nucleus splits into lighter ones.]

Q: Nuclear fusion is the process in which:
A) Light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus
B) A heavy nucleus splits into lighter nuclei
C) A nucleus emits alpha particles
D) A nucleus emits beta particles
Answer: A [Fusion: light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus.]

Q: The energy released in nuclear fission is due to:
A) The mass defect
B) The binding energy
C) The kinetic energy
D) The potential energy
Answer: A [Energy released comes from the mass defect (E = delta_m c^2).]

Q: The nuclear fission of U-235 is initiated by:
A) A slow neutron
B) A fast neutron
C) A proton
D) An alpha particle
Answer: A [U-235 fission is induced by slow (thermal) neutrons.]

Q: The products of nuclear fission of U-235 are:
A) Two lighter nuclei and neutrons
B) Two heavier nuclei
C) Alpha particles and beta particles
D) Only neutrons
Answer: A [Fission products: two smaller nuclei and some neutrons.]

Q: The chain reaction in nuclear fission is sustained by:
A) The neutrons released in the fission process
B) The energy released
C) The heat generated
D) The radiation emitted
Answer: A [Neutrons released cause further fission, sustaining the chain reaction.]

Q: The moderator in a nuclear reactor is used to:
A) Slow down fast neutrons
B) Absorb neutrons
C) Cool the reactor
D) Shield the reactor
Answer: A [Moderators (e.g., graphite, heavy water) slow down neutrons.]

Q: The control rods in a nuclear reactor are made of:
A) Materials that absorb neutrons (e.g., boron, cadmium)
B) Materials that reflect neutrons
C) Materials that slow down neutrons
D) Materials that emit neutrons
Answer: A [Control rods (e.g., boron, cadmium) absorb neutrons to control the reaction rate.]

Q: The coolant in a nuclear reactor is used to:
A) Remove heat from the reactor core
B) Slow down neutrons
C) Absorb neutrons
D) Shield the reactor
Answer: A [Coolants (e.g., water, liquid sodium) carry away heat.]

Q: The fuel used in a nuclear reactor is typically:
A) U-235 or Pu-239
B) U-238
C) Th-232
D) H-2
Answer: A [U-235 and Pu-239 are common nuclear fuels.]

Q: The energy released in nuclear fusion is:
A) Greater than that in fission per unit mass
B) Less than that in fission per unit mass
C) Equal to that in fission
D) Zero
Answer: A [Fusion releases more energy per unit mass than fission.]

Q: The fusion reaction in the Sun involves:
A) Hydrogen nuclei fusing to form helium
B) Helium nuclei fusing to form carbon
C) Carbon nuclei fusing to form oxygen
D) Oxygen nuclei fusing to form iron
Answer: A [The Sun's energy comes from hydrogen fusion to helium.]

Q: The half-life of a radioactive substance is:
A) The time for half of the nuclei to decay
B) The time for all nuclei to decay
C) The time for one-fourth of the nuclei to decay
D) The time for three-fourths of the nuclei to decay
Answer: A [Half-life is the time for half the sample to decay.]

Q: The decay constant (lambda) is related to half-life (T_1/2) by:
A) T_1/2 = 0.693 / lambda
B) T_1/2 = lambda / 0.693
C) T_1/2 = 0.693 * lambda
D) T_1/2 = 1 / lambda
Answer: A [T_1/2 = ln 2 / lambda = 0.693 / lambda.]

Q: The activity of a radioactive sample is:
A) A = lambda N
B) A = N / lambda
C) A = N * lambda^2
D) A = N^2 / lambda
Answer: A [Activity = rate of decay = lambda N.]

Q: The SI unit of activity is:
A) Becquerel (Bq)
B) Curie (Ci)
C) Rutherford (Rd)
D) Gray (Gy)
Answer: A [Becquerel (1 Bq = 1 decay/s).]

Q: 1 Curie is equal to:
A) 3.7 × 10^10 Bq
B) 3.7 × 10^7 Bq
C) 3.7 × 10^13 Bq
D) 3.7 × 10^5 Bq
Answer: A [1 Ci = 3.7 × 10^10 Bq.]

Q: The law of radioactive decay is:
A) N = N0 e^(-lambda t)
B) N = N0 e^(lambda t)
C) N = N0 (1 - e^(-lambda t))
D) N = N0 (1 + e^(-lambda t))
Answer: A [N = N0 e^(-lambda t).]

Q: Alpha decay is the emission of:
A) A helium nucleus (4He)
B) An electron
C) A positron
D) A gamma ray
Answer: A [Alpha particle is a helium nucleus (2 protons + 2 neutrons).]

Q: Beta decay is the emission of:
A) An electron or positron
B) A helium nucleus
C) A gamma ray
D) A proton
Answer: A [Beta decay emits an electron (beta-) or positron (beta+).]

Q: Gamma decay is the emission of:
A) High-energy photons (gamma rays)
B) Electrons
C) Positrons
D) Helium nuclei
Answer: A [Gamma decay emits gamma rays (high-energy photons).]

Q: In beta-minus decay, the atomic number:
A) Increases by 1
B) Decreases by 1
C) Remains unchanged
D) Increases by 2
Answer: A [In beta- decay, Z increases by 1 (n -> p + e- + antineutrino).]

Q: In beta-plus decay, the atomic number:
A) Decreases by 1
B) Increases by 1
C) Remains unchanged
D) Decreases by 2
Answer: A [In beta+ decay, Z decreases by 1 (p -> n + e+ + neutrino).]

Q: In alpha decay, the atomic number:
A) Decreases by 2
B) Increases by 2
C) Decreases by 1
D) Increases by 1
Answer: A [Alpha decay reduces Z by 2 (loses 2 protons).]

Q: In alpha decay, the mass number:
A) Decreases by 4
B) Increases by 4
C) Decreases by 2
D) Increases by 2
Answer: A [Alpha decay reduces A by 4.]

Q: The carbon-14 dating method is used to determine the age of:
A) Organic materials
B) Rocks
C) Minerals
D) Inorganic materials
Answer: A [C-14 dating is used for organic materials (e.g., fossils, wood).]

Q: The half-life of carbon-14 is:
A) 5730 years
B) 5730 days
C) 5730 hours
D) 5730 seconds
Answer: A [T_1/2 of C-14 ≈ 5730 years.]

Q: The radioactive isotope used in medical imaging is:
A) Technetium-99m
B) Uranium-235
C) Plutonium-239
D) Carbon-14
Answer: A [Tc-99m is widely used in medical diagnostics.]

Q: The fission of U-235 releases about:
A) 200 MeV per fission
B) 200 J per fission
C) 200 eV per fission
D) 200 keV per fission
Answer: A [U-235 fission releases ~200 MeV energy.]

Q: The fusion of hydrogen to helium in the Sun releases energy at a rate of:
A) 3.8 × 10^26 W
B) 3.8 × 10^20 W
C) 3.8 × 10^30 W
D) 3.8 × 10^24 W
Answer: A [The Sun's luminosity is ~3.8 × 10^26 W.]

Q: The mass of a proton is approximately:
A) 1.67 × 10^-27 kg
B) 9.1 × 10^-31 kg
C) 1.6 × 10^-19 kg
D) 6.6 × 10^-34 kg
Answer: A [m_p ≈ 1.67 × 10^-27 kg.]

Q: The mass of a neutron is approximately:
A) 1.67 × 10^-27 kg
B) 9.1 × 10^-31 kg
C) 1.6 × 10^-19 kg
D) 6.6 × 10^-34 kg
Answer: A [m_n ≈ 1.67 × 10^-27 kg (slightly more than proton).]

Q: The binding energy of a nucleus is a measure of its:
A) Stability
B) Radioactivity
C) Size
D) Mass
Answer: A [Higher binding energy means more stable nucleus.]

Q: The mass defect is directly related to:
A) The binding energy
B) The atomic number
C) The mass number
D) The density
Answer: A [delta_m = E_b / c^2.]

Q: The energy equivalent of 1 atomic mass unit (amu) is:
A) 931.5 MeV
B) 931.5 eV
C) 931.5 keV
D) 931.5 J
Answer: A [1 amu = 931.5 MeV.]

Q: The atomic mass unit (amu) is defined as:
A) 1/12 of the mass of a C-12 atom
B) The mass of a proton
C) The mass of a neutron
D) The mass of an electron
Answer: A [1 amu = 1/12 mass of C-12 atom.]

Q: The mass of a C-12 atom is exactly:
A) 12 amu
B) 12 g
C) 12 kg
D) 12 mg
Answer: A [C-12 is defined as exactly 12 amu.]

Q: The proton was discovered by:
A) Rutherford

Q: The electron was discovered by:

Q: The nuclear radius is proportional to:
A) A^(1/3)
B) A^(2/3)
C) A
D) A^2
Answer: A [R ∝ A^(1/3).]

Q: The density of a nucleus is approximately:

B) 2.3 × 10^15 kg/m^3
