Q: In the Lewis structure of the ozone molecule that correctly accounts for formal charges, the formal charges on the three oxygen atoms (central, terminal double-bonded, terminal single-bonded) are respectively
A) +1, 0, –1
B) 0, +1, –1
C) +1, –1, 0
D) –1, 0, +1
Answer: A [Formal charge = valence e⁻ – non-bonding e⁻ – ½ bonding e⁻. Central O: 6 – 2 – ½(6) = +1; terminal O with double bond: 6 – 4 – ½(4) = 0; terminal O with single bond: 6 – 6 – ½(2) = –1. This is the lowest-energy set of formal charges.]

Q: Which of the following statements about resonance hybrids is rigorously correct according to the NCERT treatment?
A) The molecule spends a fraction of time in each canonical form.
B) Canonical forms are in rapid equilibrium with each other.
C) The hybrid has lower energy than any single canonical structure and the actual bond lengths are intermediate.
D) Resonance is observed only when all atoms obey the octet rule.
Answer: C [Resonance stabilises the molecule (hybrid energy lower than any contributor) and averages bond characteristics; the molecule has a single structure that cannot be represented by one Lewis formula.]

Q: The experimentally observed C–O bond length in CO₂ is 115 pm. Given that typical C=O and C≡O lengths are 121 pm and 110 pm, the best description is
A) two pure C=O double bonds
B) a resonance hybrid of three canonical forms in which the average bond order is between 2 and 3
C) two pure C≡O triple bonds
D) a structure with one C=O and one C≡O
Answer: B [The observed length lies between C=O and C≡O; three resonance forms (O=C=O, ⁻O–C≡O⁺, ⁺O≡C–O⁻) are required and the hybrid has intermediate bond order.]

Q: For the isoelectronic species N₂, CO and NO⁺, the bond orders are
A) 3, 2, 2
B) 3, 3, 3
C) 2, 3, 2
D) 3, 3, 2
Answer: B [All three possess the same number of electrons (14) and the same MO configuration giving a bond order of 3.]

Q: Which pair correctly matches the molecule/ion with its formal-charge description that is preferred by the formal-charge criterion?
A) CO₃²⁻ – carbon has formal charge zero
B) NO₂⁻ – nitrogen has formal charge +1 in both resonance forms
C) O₃ – all oxygen atoms have formal charge zero
D) SO₂ – sulphur has formal charge +2
Answer: B [In both resonance structures of NO₂⁻ the nitrogen carries formal charge +1 while one oxygen is zero and the other –1; the other listed statements are incorrect.]

Q: The lattice enthalpy of NaCl is 788 kJ mol⁻¹. Given IE₁(Na) = 495.8 kJ mol⁻¹ and Δ_egH(Cl) = –348.7 kJ mol⁻¹, the enthalpy change for the hypothetical gas-phase process Na⁺(g) + Cl⁻(g) → NaCl(s) is
A) –788 kJ mol⁻¹
B) –147.1 kJ mol⁻¹
C) +147.1 kJ mol⁻¹
D) –935.1 kJ mol⁻¹
Answer: A [Lattice enthalpy is defined as the energy required to separate the solid into gaseous ions; the reverse process therefore releases –788 kJ mol⁻¹.]

Q: According to Fajans' rules, the ionic compound that is expected to show the greatest covalent character is
A) LiF
B) CsF
C) LiI
D) CsI
Answer: C [Smallest cation + largest anion maximises polarisation; Li⁺ is the smallest cation and I⁻ the largest anion among the options.]

Q: The dipole moment of NF₃ is much smaller than that of NH₃ even though F is more electronegative than N. The correct reason is
A) NF₃ is planar while NH₃ is pyramidal
B) the orbital dipole of the lone pair on N opposes the N–F bond dipoles
C) the N–F bonds are non-polar
D) NF₃ has no lone pair
Answer: B [In NF₃ the lone-pair orbital dipole is directed opposite to the resultant of the three N–F bond dipoles, reducing the net moment; in NH₃ the two contributions reinforce each other.]

Q: For the polyatomic molecule H₂O the successive bond-dissociation enthalpies are 502 kJ mol⁻¹ and 427 kJ mol⁻¹. The mean O–H bond enthalpy is therefore
A) 502 kJ mol⁻¹
B) 427 kJ mol⁻¹
C) 464.5 kJ mol⁻¹
D) 929 kJ mol⁻¹
Answer: C [Mean bond enthalpy = (502 + 427)/2 = 464.5 kJ mol⁻¹.]

Q: The bond order of O₂⁺ is
A) 1.5
B) 2
C) 2.5
D) 3
Answer: C [O₂ has bond order 2; removal of an antibonding electron raises the bond order to 2.5.]

Q: In the VSEPR treatment of SF₄ the preferred geometry places the lone pair
A) in an axial position because axial positions have more space
B) in an equatorial position because this arrangement minimises the number of 90° lp–bp interactions
C) in either position with equal energy
D) in a position that forces a square-planar geometry
Answer: B [Equatorial placement gives only two 90° lp–bp contacts; axial placement would give three, so the equatorial (see-saw) arrangement is lower in energy.]

Q: The molecule ClF₃ has the geometry
A) trigonal planar
B) T-shaped
C) tetrahedral
D) square planar
Answer: B [Five electron pairs (3 bp + 2 lp) adopt a trigonal-bipyramidal arrangement; the two lone pairs occupy equatorial positions, producing a T-shaped molecular geometry.]

Q: Which of the following species is expected to be square pyramidal?
A) XeF₄
B) BrF₅
C) SF₄
D) PCl₅
Answer: B [BrF₅ has six electron pairs (5 bp + 1 lp); the lone pair occupies an axial position of the octahedron, giving a square-pyramidal molecular shape.]

Q: The observed H–N–H angle in NH₃ is 107° rather than the ideal tetrahedral angle because
A) the N–H bonds are longer than expected
B) lp–bp repulsion is greater than bp–bp repulsion
C) nitrogen uses pure p orbitals
D) the molecule is planar
Answer: B [Greater lp–bp repulsion compresses the H–N–H angles from 109.5° to 107°.]

Q: In the series BeCl₂, BCl₃, CCl₄, the hybridisation of the central atom changes as
A) sp → sp² → sp³
B) sp² → sp³ → sp³d
C) sp → sp³ → sp³d²
D) sp² → sp → sp³
Answer: A [BeCl₂ is linear (sp), BCl₃ is trigonal planar (sp²), CCl₄ is tetrahedral (sp³).]

Q: The hybridisation of the central atom in XeF₄ is
A) sp³
B) sp³d
C) sp³d²
D) d²sp³
Answer: C [Six electron pairs (4 bp + 2 lp) require octahedral hybridisation (sp³d²); the two lone pairs occupy opposite vertices, giving a square-planar molecular geometry.]

Q: In ethene the carbon–carbon bond consists of
A) one σ and one π bond formed by pure p orbitals
B) one sp²–sp² σ bond and one p–p π bond
C) two sp²–sp² σ bonds
D) one sp–sp σ bond and two π bonds
Answer: B [Each carbon is sp²-hybridised; the σ framework is formed by sp² orbitals and the π bond by side-ways overlap of the remaining unhybridised p orbitals.]

Q: The hybridisation of phosphorus in PCl₅ (gas phase) is
A) sp³
B) sp³d
C) sp³d²
D) d²sp³
Answer: B [Five electron pairs around P require trigonal-bipyramidal hybridisation (sp³d).]

Q: Which statement about the relative strength of σ and π bonds is correct?
A) A π bond is always stronger than a σ bond because of greater overlap
B) A σ bond is stronger than a π bond because of greater extent of axial overlap
C) Both bonds have identical strength
D) Strength depends only on the atoms involved, not on the type of overlap
Answer: B [Axial (end-on) overlap of a σ bond is more effective than the lateral overlap of a π bond; hence σ bonds are stronger.]

Q: The molecule that contains both sp³ and sp² hybridised carbon atoms is
